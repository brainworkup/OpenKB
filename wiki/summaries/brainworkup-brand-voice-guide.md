# brainworkup — Brand Voice Guide

**Source:** brainworkup-brand-voice-guide
**Version:** 1.1.0 | **Owner:** Joey Trampush, PhD

## Overview

This guide defines the voice, tone, messaging, terminology, and style standards for brainworkup, a pediatric and forensic neuropsychology practice led by Joey Trampush, PhD (USC Keck School of Medicine). It governs all written communication across clinical, legal, academic, and public-facing channels, and connects directly to [[concepts/brand-voice-strategy]].

## Brand Personality

brainworkup's core persona is a **trusted authority figure** — a clinician-scientist who combines deep academic credibility with accessibility. In pediatric contexts, the brand is warm and reassuring; in forensic contexts, it shifts to a **meticulous expert witness** — precise, measured, and unimpeachable.

Credentials (20+ years experience, NIH-funded research) serve to reassure, never to intimidate.

## Four Voice Attributes

| Attribute | Is | Is Not |
|---|---|---|
| **Authoritative** | Research-informed, precise, experienced | Condescending, jargon-heavy |
| **Empathic** | Warm, attuned to family anxiety | Sentimental, patronizing |
| **Clear** | Clinically precise yet accessible | Dumbed-down or vague |
| **Evidence-Based** | Grounded in peer-reviewed research | Dogmatic or over-hedged |

## Audiences

### Primary
- **Parents and families** — often anxious, first-time evaluees; need clear next steps and partnership, not just data.
- **Referring providers** (pediatricians, psychiatrists, educators) — expect professional-level, concise, actionable findings.

### Secondary
- **Attorneys and courts** — forensic context; require evidentiary precision, formal objectivity.
- **Academic/research colleagues** — expect methodological rigor and disciplinary sophistication.

## Core Messaging Pillars

1. **Clinical expertise grounded in science** — bridges cognitive genomics, psychiatric genetics, and neurodevelopment with clinical practice.
2. **Evaluations that lead to action** — every report produces specific, practical, individualized recommendations tied to the [[concepts/neuropsychological-reporting]] process.
3. **Respect for the developing mind** — pediatric specialization; developmental context changes how data is interpreted.
4. **Dual clinical and forensic expertise** — serves both families and legal proceedings.

## Tone Spectrum

Voice attributes stay constant; **tone** shifts by context:

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Pediatric / Family | Warmth ↑, formality ↓, encouragement ↑ |
| Forensic / Legal | Precision ↑, warmth ↓, formality ↑ |
| Professional / Referral | Efficiency ↑, clinical precision ↑, warmth moderate |
| Public / Marketing | Approachability ↑, authority ↑ |
| Academic / Research | Rigor ↑, precision ↑, warmth low |

The shifts between these registers are a practical example of [[concepts/clinical-communication-register]] in action across audience types.

## Style Rules

- **Oxford comma:** Always used.
- **Headings:** Sentence case.
- **Contractions:** Yes in parent-facing copy; never in forensic/legal writing.
- **Numbers:** Spell out one–nine; numerals for 10+.
- **Percent:** `%` in data contexts; "percent" in narrative prose.
- **Exclamation marks:** Max one per piece; never in forensic or clinical reports.
- **Emoji:** Only on Instagram; never in reports, LinkedIn, provider emails, or website.
- **Bold** for key terms in parent-facing content; *italics* for test names and technical terms.
- Inline `code` for test abbreviations in technical/research contexts only — never parent-facing.

## Brand Wordmark

- Always **lowercase**, always one word: **brainworkup** (inspired by `bell hooks`, `thoughtbot`, `deadmau5`).
- Never capitalize, even at the start of a sentence — restructure instead: "At brainworkup, we…"
- Possessive: `brainworkup's`.
- Legal/practitioner name: "Joey Trampush, PhD" (standard capitalization).

## Key Terminology

| Preferred | Avoid |
|---|---|
| neuropsychological evaluation | neuropsych testing, brain test |
| cognitive profile | IQ score, intelligence level |
| differences (parent-facing) | deficits (parent-facing) |
| challenges, areas for support | weaknesses, problems |
| recommendations | suggestions |
| evidence-based | cutting-edge, revolutionary |
| average range | normal |
| had difficulty with | failed |
| children and adolescents | pediatric patients (parent-facing) |

### Terms to Avoid Entirely
"world-class," "cutting-edge," "holistic," "revolutionary," "simple/easy" (re: evaluation), "normal" (re: scores), "failed" (re: performance), "disorder" (in headlines/social media).

The terminology guide also governs how [[concepts/neuropsychological-test-scores]] are communicated to different audiences, and how [[concepts/cognitive-domains]] such as executive functioning, working memory, and processing speed are described for parents versus providers versus courts.

## Inclusive Language

- Gender-neutral (they/them) when pronouns are unknown.
- Person-first language by default: "a child with ADHD"; identity-first when community-preferred (e.g., "autistic individuals").
- Avoid ableist language (e.g., replace "blind spot" → "gap").
- Scores described as a snapshot of performance, not fixed traits.
- Mindful of diverse cultural, linguistic, and socioeconomic backgrounds.

## Visual Identity (Reference)

The voice guide works alongside the visual identity system documented in [[concepts/brand-theming]] and [[concepts/brand-color-system]]:

- **Pediatric palette:** Teal (#2A9D8F), Coral (#E07A5F), Cream (#FAF3E0) — warm and approachable.
- **Forensic/academic palette:** Navy (#1B365D), Gold (#C9A94E), Ivory (#FFFDF5) — authoritative.
- **Typography** (see [[concepts/brand-typography]]): Merriweather (headings, scholarly authority), Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (body, accessibility), JetBrains Mono (data/code).
- Design mirrors voice: authoritative yet accessible.

## Quick Reference

> **Voice:** Trusted, evidence-based, empathic, clear.
> **Core message:** "Here's what the data shows, here's what it means, and here's what to do next."
> **Parents:** Warm authority — reassure, explain, recommend.
> **Providers:** Collegial precision — concise, actionable, rigorous.
> **Court:** Measured expertise — every word defensible.
> **Public:** Inviting credibility — quality content demonstrates expertise.
