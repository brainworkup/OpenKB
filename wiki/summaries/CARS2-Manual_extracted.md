# CARS2 Manual — Childhood Autism Rating Scale, Second Edition

**Authors:** Eric Schopler, Mary E. Van Bourgondien, G. Janette Wellman, Steven R. Love
**Publisher:** Western Psychological Services (2010, Second Edition)

## Overview

The Childhood Autism Rating Scale, Second Edition (CARS2) is a standardized clinical instrument for identifying and characterizing [[concepts/autism-spectrum-disorder-clinical-features]] symptoms. It consists of three forms:

- **CARS2-ST** (Standard Version): For individuals under age 6, or age 6+ with IQ ≤ 79 or notably impaired communication
- **CARS2-HF** (High-Functioning Version): For individuals age 6+ with IQ ≥ 80 and fluent communication
- **CARS2-QPC** (Questionnaire for Parents or Caregivers): Unscored caregiver information form

## Rating Structure

Both the CARS2-ST and CARS2-HF each contain **15 items** rated on a 1–4 scale (with half-point increments), yielding a Total raw score from 15–60. Ratings assess behavior relative to typically developing peers of the same age, considering **peculiarity, frequency, intensity, and duration**.

### CARS2-ST Items
1. Relating to People
2. Imitation
3. Emotional Response
4. Body Use
5. Object Use
6. Adaptation to Change
7. Visual Response
8. Listening Response
9. Taste, Smell, and Touch Response and Use
10. Fear or Nervousness
11. Verbal Communication
12. Nonverbal Communication
13. Activity Level
14. Level and Consistency of Intellectual Response
15. General Impressions

### CARS2-HF Items (Key Differences)
- Item 1: Social-Emotional Understanding (new)
- Item 2: Emotional Expression and Regulation of Emotions (modified)
- Item 6: Adaptation to Change/Restricted Interests
- Item 13: Thinking/Cognitive Integration Skills (new; replaces Activity Level)
- Items 2 (Imitation) and 13 (Activity Level) from CARS2-ST dropped

## Score Interpretation

### CARS2-ST Cutoffs
| Score (ages 0–12) | Score (ages 13+) | Category |
|---|---|---|
| 15–29.5 | 15–27.5 | Likely nonautistic |
| 30–36.5 | 28–34.5 | Mild-to-moderate ASD symptoms |
| 37–60 | 35–60 | Severe ASD symptoms |

### CARS2-HF Cutoffs
| Score | Category |
|---|---|
| 15–27.5 | Likely nonautistic |
| 28–33.5 | Mild-to-moderate ASD symptoms |
| 34–60 | Severe ASD symptoms |

### T-Scores
Total raw scores convert to T-scores (mean=50, SD=10) calibrated on a clinical sample of 2,000+ individuals with autism diagnoses. These reflect relative severity compared to the autism clinical population, not the general population.

## Psychometric Properties

- **Internal consistency:** α = .93 (CARS2-ST), α = .96 (CARS2-HF)
- **Interrater reliability (Total score):** r = .84 (original CARS), r = .95 (CARS2-HF)
- **Test-retest stability:** r = .88 over ~1 year
- **SEM:** 0.68 raw score points (CARS2-ST); 0.73 raw score points (CARS2-HF); ~2.7–2.8 T-score points for both
- **Development samples:** Original CARS N=1,606; CARS2-ST verification N=1,034; CARS2-HF development N=994

### Validity
- CARS2-ST sensitivity = .88, specificity = .86 vs. clinical diagnosis (cutoff = 30)
- CARS2-HF AUC = .93 vs. all nonautism groups; sensitivity = .81, specificity = .87
- Correlates with ADOS Total score (r=.77–.79), SRS (r=.38–.47), and expert clinical ratings (r=.80–.84)
- Factor analysis yields 2 factors for CARS2-ST (communication/sensory; emotional) and 3 factors for CARS2-HF (social-emotional; cognitive/verbal; sensory)

## Theoretical Basis

Items are grounded in five major diagnostic systems: Kanner (1943), Creak (1961), Rutter (1978), NSAC (1978), and DSM (all editions). The CARS2-HF additionally addresses:

- [[concepts/theory-of-mind]] deficits (social perspective taking, understanding others' emotions)
- Weak central coherence (focus on details with difficulty integrating into meaning)
- [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]] (attention shifting, flexibility, planning)
- Scattered cognitive profiles and savant skills
- Emotional regulation and anxiety
- Sensory sensitivities and motor difficulties

## Administration Guidelines

- **CARS2-ST** can be completed from a single source (direct observation, parent interview, or chart review)
- **CARS2-HF** requires multiple sources; direct observation plus collateral informant (parent, teacher) is mandatory for Items 2, 8, 9, 10
- **Parents should NOT complete the rating booklets**; use CARS2-QPC and direct interview instead
- CARS2-ST and CARS2-HF results alone are insufficient for diagnosis; comprehensive evaluation including developmental history is required

The CARS2 is one of several [[concepts/autism-diagnostic-rating-scales]] and functions as a [[concepts/behavioral-rating-scales]] instrument within a broader [[concepts/neuropsychological-assessment-pipeline]].

## Case Examples

- **Michael** (4-year-old, CARS2-ST): Total raw score = 36.5, T-score = 48, Mild-to-Moderate category; diagnosed with autism
- **Daniel** (8-year-old, CARS2-HF): Total raw score = 37, T-score = 55, Severe category; diagnosed with high-functioning autism

## Intervention Planning

Item ratings map onto five intervention domains (Tables 12 & 13):
1. Social Interaction
2. Communication
3. Restricted Interests and Stereotyped Behavior
4. Sensory Issues and Associated Features
5. Thinking Style and Cognitive Issues

The manual recommends [[concepts/structured-teaching-teacch]] (Division TEACCH's approach) as a foundational intervention framework, supplemented by resources for social skills, communication (PECS, SCERTS, Hanen), sensory integration, and cognitive-behavioral strategies.

## Diagnostic Feedback to Parents

Chapter 4 provides detailed guidance on communicating results, including a two-dimensional severity model (cognitive/language dimension × autism severity dimension) to clarify functioning levels. Emphasizes balanced presentation of strengths and weaknesses and individualized [[concepts/clinical-communication-register]]. The process mirrors broader [[concepts/clinical-report-structure]] principles of integrating quantitative findings with narrative context. The CARS2-ST and CARS2-HF are positioned as tools to support [[concepts/neurodevelopmental-clinical-intake]] by characterizing functional profiles across domains including [[concepts/cognitive-domains]] and [[concepts/speech-language-development-disorders]].

## Historical Context

Originally developed as the Childhood Psychosis Rating Scale (1971) at UNC Chapel Hill for Division TEACCH; retitled CARS in 1988. The CARS2 (2010) adds the HF form, standard scores, the QPC, and updated intervention resources while preserving backward compatibility of CARS2-ST scores with the original CARS.
