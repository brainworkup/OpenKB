# Summary: Recommendations for Attention Problems

**Source:** bwu.neuro.reports.recs.attention-problems

## Overview

This document provides a comprehensive set of recommendations and structured intervention strategies for children experiencing attention problems, defined as chronic and severe inconsistencies in the ability to maintain and regulate focus. Characteristics include distractibility, inability to concentrate, disorganization, failure to complete tasks, and poor study skills. The content is written in a neuropsychological report template style using placeholder references (e.g., `ref:first-name`).

## Key Definitions

- **Attention Problems:** Chronic, severe inability to maintain focus; driven by both internal and external distractions, even in quiet environments.
- **Target Population:** School-age children, addressed across home and classroom settings.

## Practical Recommendations

### Environmental Modifications
- Eliminate distractions and extraneous noise at school and home.
- Provide a quiet, supervised workspace.
- Seat student away from other students when needed.
- Establish a structured learning environment with consistent physical arrangement, schedule, and behavioral expectations.

### Movement and Sensory Strategies
- **Motor breaks:** 3–5 minutes of physical movement after every 15–20 minutes of cognitive effort.
- **Stretch breaks:** Allow standing during extended cognitive tasks.
- **Wiggle seats / stress balls:** Permit fidgeting in a socially appropriate manner to relieve excess physical energy.
- Allow students to stand during seatwork or perform errands as reinforcement.

### Instructional Strategies
- Use multiple teaching modalities to prevent extended reliance on one sensory channel.
- Mix high- and low-interest tasks (e.g., follow lecture with hands-on activity).
- Break long/complex tasks into smaller, sequential steps.
- Use highlighting or underlining to emphasize key directions.
- Establish eye contact before giving instructions.
- Provide guided notes and instruction in attending to relevant information.

### Behavioral and Self-Management Techniques
- **Self-talk:** Inner reminders (e.g., "Sit up straight, eyes on the speaker").
- **[[concepts/self-monitoring-behavior]]:** Track on/off-task behavior at timed intervals; calculate percentages; gradually increase interval length.
- **Prearranged non-punitive cues:** E.g., a tap on the shoulder to redirect; discussed with student beforehand.
- **Proofreading:** Encourage review of work hours or days after completion; offer partial credit for corrections.
- Teach self-advocacy: requesting extended time, removing distractions.

### Memory and Organization
- Encourage starting with main ideas, then classifying new information into clusters by meaning.
- Assign a peer buddy for questions and task clarification.
- Use small group or peer tutoring formats.

## Intervention Frameworks

### Intervention Option 1: Daily Behavior Report Cards (DBRCs)
A structured, behaviorally-based system for tracking and reinforcing attention-related behaviors daily, aligned with [[concepts/behavior-modification]] principles.

**Core Elements:**
1. Define target behaviors.
2. Monitor and record daily.
3. Provide reinforcement for meeting targets.
4. Communicate results to children and parents.

**Implementation Phases:**
- **PREP:** Identify behaviors and raters; create a letter-grade rating system with behavioral anchors.
- **IMPLEMENT:** Begin on a set day; provide feedback; consider graphing progress; use check-in/check-out system; reward goal attainment.
- **EVALUATE:** Compare pre/post ratings; ensure reinforcement is consistent; reassess reinforcer and feedback quality.

### Intervention Option 2: Modified Task-Presentation Strategies
Antecedent instructional modifications to increase task interest and time-on-task, informed by [[concepts/functional-behavioral-assessment]].

**Modification Options:**
1. Offering choice of instructional activities.
2. Providing guided notes.
3. Using high-interest and hands-on activities.
4. Modifying in-class assignments and responses.
5. Modifying homework.
6. Highlighting relevant material with colors, symbols, or font changes.

**Implementation Phases:**
- **PREP:** Use assessment/observation data; identify differences from typical instruction; prepare materials.
- **IMPLEMENT:** Present task using the modified strategy.
- **EVALUATE:** Observe attention and performance; continue effective modifications, discontinue ineffective ones.
