# Summary: Apply-to-Y-Combinator-JWT

## Overview
This document is a draft **Y Combinator Summer 2026 application** for a company tentatively centered on **Luria**, a **local-first, agent-based system** for automating the neuropsychological evaluation workflow. The application is largely incomplete, but it contains substantial detail on founder background, origin story, product direction, workflow automation, and market positioning.

## Founder
- **Founder:** Joey Trampush
- Joey states that he has written **all of the code** and handles all technical work.
- He notes that **AI has assisted** him in the process.
- He is **not explicitly seeking a cofounder**, but remains open depending on the role.

## Product Direction
The core product described is **Luria**, named after **A.R. Luria**, presented as a system that can:
- operate as a **team of agents and subagents**
- work **almost fully autonomously**
- execute a clinical workflow "from soup to nuts"
- collect and organize data from multiple sources
- analyze neuropsychological data
- generate comprehensive reports
- keep sensitive data **local and secure**

## Development History
Joey describes a roughly **3-year evolution** of the system:
- initial work began with using **R** and **RMarkdown** for clinical work
- early functions focused on extracting and organizing data from **CSV files**, then **PDFs**, and eventually **arbitrary file types**
- this expanded into many R functions and supporting packages
- the workflow later incorporated **LaTeX**, [[concepts/quarto]], and now Typst for report writing, tables, and plots
- development was accelerated by **GitHub Copilot** and later **LLMs**

Time commitment:
- **~2.5 years part-time**
- **past 6 months full-time**
- **~3 years total**

## Origin of the Idea
The founder frames the idea as emerging directly from his own professional constraints rather than from abstract startup ideation.

Relevant background:
- researcher in **neurodevelopment** and **psychiatric genetics** at USC
- **pediatric neuropsychologist**
- founder of **brainworkup**, a Los Angeles-based pediatric and forensic neuropsychology practice

Triggering problem:
- after the pandemic, patient volume increased dramatically
- faculty practice referrals reportedly grew from **~5K/month to >20K/month**
- clinical evaluation and reporting became impossible to sustain manually at prior standards

The document emphasizes that neuropsychological evaluation remains labor-intensive and historically conservative in methodology. A standard evaluation may take **4–8 hours**, while pediatric and forensic cases can take **2–3x longer**. Report writing is identified as both:
- the most time-consuming step
- the most important clinical deliverable

This is framed as a strong case for domain expertise and founder-problem fit.

## Problem Being Solved
The document identifies a specific bottleneck in neuropsychology:
- reports require integrating data from test batteries, interviews, and observations
- interpretation must span **neurocognitive** and **neurobehavioral** domains
- current reporting is highly manual, slow, and difficult to scale
- sensitive patient data creates strong constraints around privacy and deployment architecture

The product thesis is that generic AI tools are insufficient because they do not adequately handle:
- **clinical voice**
- **cross-domain interpretation**
- **strict PHI boundaries**

## Competitive Positioning
The founder claims there are **no direct competitors** automating the full neuropsychological evaluation workflow at the same level of sophistication.

Competitors mentioned indirectly:
- major test publishers such as **Pearson** and **PARInc**

Critique of incumbent tools:
- reports are **mass produced**
- tools are tied to specific tests, batteries, or rating scales
- formatting is generic
- interpretation lacks meaningful integration across domains

Claimed differentiation:
- deeper synthesis across neurocognitive and neurobehavioral findings
- preservation of authentic **clinical voice**
- **local-first architecture** designed for sensitive evaluations

This suggests possible future concepts:
- vertical AI
- [[concepts/neuropsychological-reporting]]
- local AI for healthcare

## Key Takeaways
- This is an early but detailed founder narrative for **Luria**, an AI system for automating neuropsychological evaluations.
- The strongest parts of the application are the founder’s **domain expertise**, **pain-driven origin story**, and clear articulation of why the workflow is unusually difficult to automate well.
- The product’s defining characteristics are **local-first deployment**, **agentic workflow execution**, and **high-quality integrated report generation**.
- The application currently lacks concrete business details such as traction, revenue, legal structure, and explicit go-to-market strategy.
