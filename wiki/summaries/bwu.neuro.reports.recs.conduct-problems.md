# Conduct Problems — Intervention Recommendations

**Source:** bwu.neuro.reports.recs.conduct-problems

## Overview

This document provides clinical recommendations for addressing **conduct problems** as a primary behavioral concern. Conduct problems involve a repetitive and persistent pattern of behavior violating others' rights or age-appropriate societal norms. The document outlines evidence-based intervention strategies and detailed procedural guidance for two primary options: [[concepts/social-skills-training]] and [[concepts/parent-training]].

## Nature of Conduct Problems

- Among the most significant and challenging behavioral/emotional problems in children
- Characterized by resistance to change, partly due to the intrinsically rewarding nature of antisocial behaviors
- Require both **prevention** (for at-risk children) and **treatment** (for identified cases) to interrupt disorder progression
- Subject of concern in neuropsychological assessment and behavioral reporting contexts (see [[concepts/conduct-problems-interventions]])

## Evidence-Based Intervention Strategies

Multiple approaches have demonstrated effectiveness:

- [[concepts/anger-management]] Skills Training
- Independent and Interdependent Group-Oriented Contingency Management
- Moral Motivation Training
- Multimodal Interventions
- Multisystemic Therapy
- **[[concepts/parent-training]]**
- **Problem-Solving Training**
- **[[concepts/social-skills-training]]**

## Intervention Option 1: Social Skills Training

A cognitive-behavioral approach (see [[concepts/behavior-modification]]) teaching prosocial skills as alternatives to maladaptive behaviors.

### Goal
Prevent and remediate conduct problems by building social competency and prosocial skill use.

### Essential Elements
1. Determine social skills deficits
2. Demonstrate appropriate skills via explanation and modeling
3. Apply skills in contrived (role-play) scenarios
4. Provide feedback and reinforcement
5. Apply skills in actual situations

### Key Procedural Steps

**PREP**
- Form small groups with similar presenting problems
- Establish performance goals, boundaries, rules
- Identify and prioritize social skills deficits via assessment, teacher/parent input, and self-report

**IMPLEMENT**
- Introduce one skill per session with visual supports
- Use brainstorming, modeling, and role-play
- Review previously learned skills at each session
- Use journals to track skill use and outcomes
- Hold periodic refresher sessions

**EVALUATE**
- Distribute skill steps to teachers and parents for monitoring
- Schedule routine classroom observations

## Intervention Option 2: Parent Training

A psychoeducational/social learning intervention targeting parent behavior and thought processes to improve child outcomes.

### Goal
Decrease antisocial behavior by increasing effective parenting skills and positive disciplinary techniques; break the **coercive parent-child interaction cycle**.

### Essential Elements
Parents are coached in:
- Understanding coercive interaction cycles
- Reinforcement strategies (verbal praise, social, tangible, activity reinforcers)
- Observation and play skills
- Response-cost, timeout, punishment, and extinction procedures
- Relationship enhancement (communication, problem-solving)
- Token economy and reward charts; contingency contracts
- Mood management, self-determination, relaxation, stress reduction
- [[concepts/anger-management]] techniques and [[concepts/self-monitoring-behavior]]

### Key Procedural Steps

**PREP**
- Conduct intake/family climate assessment
- Identify skill deficits and formulate goals
- Determine format, location, timing, and number of sessions
- Consider a partnership contract

**IMPLEMENT**
- Teach one technique per session with verbal description, discussion, and modeling
- Role-play with parents (and child when appropriate)
- Assign homework: independent technique application and documentation
- Begin each session reviewing prior technique and homework

**EVALUATE**
- Monitor parent training uptake and application
- Continue training at the rate/duration needed to sustain effectiveness
