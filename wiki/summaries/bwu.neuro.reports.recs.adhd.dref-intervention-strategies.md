# D-REF Intervention Strategies

**Source:** bwu.neuro.reports.recs.adhd.dref-intervention-strategies

## Overview

This document provides educator-facing intervention strategies tied to five cognitive and behavioral indices assessed by the D-REF (Diagnostic Rating and Evaluation Form). Strategies are organized by index and activated at specific T-score thresholds (≥60 or ≥70), allowing for tiered, severity-sensitive intervention planning. It is designed for use in [[concepts/adhd-classroom-interventions]] and supports [[concepts/neuropsychological-reporting]].

---

## Indices and Strategies

### 1. Attention/Working Memory Index (T ≥ 60)

Targets difficulties with sustained attention and [[concepts/working-memory]] in academic settings:

- Shorten assignments to match attention span
- Provide both written and oral instructions
- Use visible timers for self-monitoring
- Break long tasks into smaller, manageable parts
- Give one assignment at a time; allow extra time
- Use private cueing signals to redirect attention
- Strategic seating (near teacher, role model, or "study buddy")
- Send written homework assignments home or post online

See also: [[concepts/working-memory-index]] | [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]]

---

### 2. Activity/Impulse Control Index (T ≥ 60)

Targets mild-to-moderate hyperactivity and impulsivity:

- Increase immediacy of rewards and consequences
- Praise and reinforce compliant behavior
- Ignore minor inappropriate behavior
- Use prudent reprimands; avoid criticism or lecturing
- Teach self-monitoring of impulsive behaviors (e.g., calling out)
- Closer supervision during transitions
- Seat near teacher or positive role model

---

### 3. Activity/Impulse Control Index (T ≥ 70)

Escalated strategies for more significant impulse control difficulties:

- Implement a formal classroom [[concepts/behavior-modification]] system
- Use weekly/monthly calendars with reinforcement markers (e.g., stickers)
- Gradually increase the interval before reinforcement is delivered
- Use a hierarchy of rewards (small rewards for short compliance periods; preferred activities for longer periods)

---

### 4. Compliance/Anger Control Index (T ≥ 60)

Targets early signs of frustration, anger dysregulation, and social withdrawal:

- Monitor for stress buildup; reduce workload proactively
- Reinforce frequently when frustration signs appear
- Encourage student to seek an adult when feeling angry
- Speak softly in non-threatening manner
- Provide leadership and responsibility opportunities to build peer esteem
- Monitor social interactions; encourage peer engagement for withdrawn students
- Maintain parent conferences to leverage outside interests and strengths

---

### 5. Compliance/Anger Control Index (T ≥ 70)

Escalated strategies for significant emotional dysregulation:

- Formal behavior management system targeting positive emotional expression
- Calendar-based reinforcement of emotional regulation
- Gradual fading of reinforcement intervals
- Reward hierarchy for sustained positive emotional expression

---

### 6. Abstract/Conceptual Reasoning Index (T ≥ 60)

Targets difficulties with organization, reasoning, and conceptual thinking:

- Use highlightable texts
- Teach explicit strategies for forming and organizing conceptual ideas
- Use sticky notes to externalize and organize ideas for essays
- Use weekly/monthly planners; review regularly
- Break assignments into ordered steps
- Involve parents in monitoring homework approach
- Continually review use of logic and reasoning in assignments

See also: [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]] | [[concepts/iep-accommodations]]

## Key Themes

| Theme | Indices Involved |
|---|---|
| Tiered intervention (T ≥ 60 vs. T ≥ 70) | Impulse Control, Anger Control |
| Behavioral reinforcement systems | Impulse Control, Anger Control |
| Environmental/seating accommodations | Attention, Impulse Control |
| Parent involvement | Attention, Anger Control |
| Self-monitoring strategies | Attention, Impulse Control |
| Organizational scaffolding | Abstract Reasoning |

## Clinical Context

These strategies are typically included in neuropsychological or psychoeducational reports as recommendations for teachers and school staff. The T-score cutoffs align with standard psychometric conventions where T ≥ 60 indicates mildly elevated scores and T ≥ 70 indicates clinically significant elevation. This document is part of a broader [[concepts/adhd-classroom-interventions]] framework used in school-based neuropsychological evaluations, and relates closely to [[concepts/neuropsychological-score-interpretation]] and [[concepts/behavioral-rating-scales]].
