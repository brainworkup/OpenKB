# PAI: General (pages 2–2)

Summary: # PAI: General

![img-0.jpeg](img-0.jpeg)

- Released in 1991
- Intended to compete with the MMPI-2 as it was not a solid tool psychometrically
- Self-Administered, 344 Likert type items forming 22 nonoverlapping scales
  - 4 validity scales, 11 clinical scales, 5 treatments scales and 2 interpersonal scales
  - Most scales can be broken down further

# PAI: General Con’t (pages 3–3)

Summary: # PAI: General Con’t

- Uses Linear T-Scores
- Norm group of college students, clinical sample, community persons and census matched
  - Community sample is SES matched for 1995
- Reading level is 4th to 6th grade
- Adults only: 40 minutes
- 5 Broad Areas:
  - validity, clinical, interpersonal style, treatment complications &subject environment

# Validity Indicators (pages 17–17)

Summary: The text outlines four validity scales (INC, INF, NIM, and PIM) used to assess the reliability of test responses. Each scale defines the purpose of the items, provides T-score thresholds for interpreting results (ranging from valid to invalid), and notes a general constraint regarding the maximum number of blank or mismarked items allowed.

# Clinical Scales (pages 21–21)

Summary: This text outlines the scoring interpretation for clinical scales using linear T-scores, defining difficulty levels ranging from 59T to 82T+. It also provides a specific overview of the 24-item Somatic Complaints (SOM) scale, detailing its three subscales and the clinical significance of its T-score ranges.

## • SOM Continued (pages 23–23)

Summary: ## • SOM Continued

- 70T or more significant concerns and impairment from somatic concerns. Unhappy and pessimistic
- 88T or more indicates a wide array of somatic concerns, involving several biological systems. In most cases these people will have diagnosable somatoform disorders. Little insight., poor prognosis.

## • **Anxiety** (ANX) (24 items): (pages 24–24)

Summary: # • **Anxiety** (ANX) (24 items):

- Items focus on phenomenology and observable signs of anxiety with an emphasis on assessment across different response modalities. 3 subscales are: Cognitive, Affective and Physiological.
- 59T or less reflects a person with few complaints of anxiety, worry or tension.
- 60T to 69T indicates some worry, sensitivity and/or tension.

### Clinical Scales: Anxiety, Anxiety-Related Disorders, and Depression (pages 24–24)

Summary: This text outlines the PAI scales for Anxiety (ANX), Anxiety-Related Disorders (ARD), and Depression (DEP). It details the subscales for each, provides clinical interpretations for T-score ranges (from low distress to severe impairment), and explains how to differentiate between generalized anxiety and specific anxiety-related conditions.

### Clinical Scales: Depression, Mania, and Paranoia (pages 28–28)

Summary: This text outlines the clinical scales for Depression, Mania, and Paranoia within the PAI, detailing the specific subscales for each and providing interpretive guidelines based on T-score ranges to assess symptom severity and behavioral characteristics.

### Clinical Scales: Paranoia, Schizophrenia, and Borderline Features (pages 32–32)

Summary: This text outlines three clinical scales—Paranoia (PAR), Schizophrenia (SCZ), and Borderline Features (BOR)—each consisting of 24 items. It details the specific subscales for each, describes the clinical focus of the items, and provides interpretive guidelines based on T-score ranges, illustrating how scores correlate with personality traits, social functioning, and the severity of psychological symptoms.

## • **Borderline Features (BOR) (24 items)** (pages 36–36)

Summary: This text provides a clinical breakdown of the Borderline Features (BOR) and Antisocial Features (ANT) scales, detailing their respective subscales and the behavioral implications of specific T-score ranges. It outlines how scores correlate with personality functioning, interpersonal stability, impulsivity, and potential for antisocial or self-destructive behavior.

## • **Alcohol Problems** (ALC) (12 items) (pages 42–42)

Summary: This text outlines the 12-item Alcohol Problems (ALC) scale, noting its susceptibility to denial and providing specific clinical interpretations for T-score ranges: 59T or less (minimal use), 60T–69T (regular use with potential issues), 70T+ (alcohol abuse), 84T+ (dependence), and 98T+ (extreme dependence).

## • **Drug Problems** (DRG) (12 items) (pages 44–44)

Summary: The text outlines the Drug Problems (DRG) scale, a 12-item measure of drug use and dependence that is prone to distortion. It provides a tiered interpretation of T-scores, ranging from infrequent use (below 60T) to regular use with adverse consequences (60T–69T), drug abuse (70T+), drug dependence (80T+), and extreme dependence (96T+).

# Treatment Consideration Scales (pages 47–47)

Summary: # Treatment Consideration Scales

- **Aggression** (AGG) (18 items)
  - Items tap characteristics and attitudes related to anger, hostility and aggression. This includes a history of aggression (verbal or physical) and attitudes conducive to aggressive behavior. 3 subscales include: Aggressive Attitude, Verbal Aggression and Physical Aggression. Content is obvious and so easily distorted.

Summary: This text provides clinical interpretations for AGG (Aggression) and SUI (Suicidal Ideation) scale scores. ## • *SUI Continued* (pages 51–51)

Summary: This text provides clinical interpretation guidelines for three psychological assessment scales: Suicidal Ideation (SUI), Stress (STR), and Treatment Rejection (RXR). It details specific T-score ranges for each scale, explaining the associated psychological states, risk levels, and implications for treatment motivation and patient behavior.

## • **Dominance** (DOM) (12 items) (pages 58–58)

Summary: This text defines and provides interpretive guidelines for two interpersonal scales: Dominance (DOM) and Warmth (WRM). For each scale, it details the behavioral characteristics associated with specific T-score ranges, spanning from low scores (submissive or cold) to high scores (domineering or overly dependent).

# Configural Interpretations (pages 64–64)

Summary: # Configural Interpretations

- Use of Cluster Analysis to establish 10 configurations.
  - Make more use of entire profile than does code types.
  - Calculated via series of comparisons of profile (22 scales) to the 10 cluster patterns.
  - Book was released (late 1996) on code types.

64


# Cluster Profiles (pages 65–65)

Summary: This text outlines ten distinct clinical cluster profiles, detailing specific scale elevations (such as DEP, SUI, ALC, DRG, SOM, and BOR) and associated clinical risks or features, including suicide risk, assault risk, confusion, and borderline or schizoaffective traits.

# Short Form (pages 69–69)

Summary: # Short Form

  Median Correlation of .91 with full length
- Uses Census matched sample.
- Invalid if more than 8 blank.
