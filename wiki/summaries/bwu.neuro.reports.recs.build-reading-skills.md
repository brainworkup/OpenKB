# Build Reading Skills — Recommendations

**Source:** bwu.neuro.reports.recs.build-reading-skills
**Tags:** recs, reading, dyslexia

## Overview

This document provides a comprehensive set of clinical recommendations for a student (KB) experiencing reading difficulties, likely related to [[concepts/dyslexia]] and [[concepts/phonological-processing]] deficits. Recommendations span intervention strategies, classroom accommodations, family involvement, and progress monitoring.

## Core Intervention Principles

- KB should receive an **evidence-based intervention** specifically targeting reading remediation.
- Progress should be **monitored daily or weekly** with data collection and graphing (e.g., using systems like AIMSweb).
- Interventions should be tailored continuously based on monitored progress.

## Phonological Processing

- KB demonstrates weaknesses in [[concepts/phonological-processing]] that interfere with reading and writing.
- Recommended activities include: rhyming games, blending sounds, removing sounds to form new words, and segmenting words by syllable or phoneme.
- Incorporating letters (orthography) alongside phonological tasks can reinforce skill-building (e.g., using letter cards to build and transform words).

## Orthographic Awareness

- A spelling game is recommended where KB spells the same word multiple ways while preserving pronunciation, building knowledge of letter-phoneme correspondences.
- This activity targets orthographic awareness and expands understanding of grapheme-phoneme relationships.

## Reading Fluency

- Teachers should emphasize [[concepts/reading-fluency]] over individual word analysis.
- Recommended fluency techniques: imitative reading, repeated reading, radio reading, phrase reading, paired reading, and echo reading.
- These should be combined with sight-word recognition, decoding, vocabulary development, and comprehension instruction.
- Recorded passages from high-interest texts can support fluency practice via listening and following along.

## Vocabulary and Grammar

- KB should be encouraged to log unfamiliar words and create flashcards until words become part of his sight-word vocabulary.
- Sentence-level activities: identifying correct/incorrect grammatical forms, distinguishing declarative, interrogative, compound, and negative sentences, and identifying parts of speech.
- Dialogic reading (a shared reading strategy) is recommended to build early literacy and vocabulary in an engaging way.

## Comprehension and Scaffolding

- Teachers should scaffold reading by previewing subject matter, pre-reading end-of-chapter questions and headings, and pausing to summarize after each sentence or paragraph.
- Open communication with KB about his difficulties is encouraged to build self-awareness and coping strategies.

## Accommodations

- **Additional time** on tests, quizzes, and assignments.
- **Recorded textbooks** for listening while following the text.
- **Reduction of required items** on assignments (with bonus points for extras).
- Access to **high-interest, low-readability books** appropriate to his reading level.
- Maintenance of a reading log/reward system to build motivation and sense of accomplishment.
- See also [[concepts/iep-accommodations]] for related accommodation frameworks.

## Digital and External Resources

- KB should be taught to independently search for digital reading tools that are both fun and educational.
- Families and teachers are directed to the **International Dyslexia Association (IDA)** and **Learning Disabilities of America (LDA)** for additional resources.
