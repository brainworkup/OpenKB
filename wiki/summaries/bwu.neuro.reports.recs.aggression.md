# Aggression Interventions — Neuropsychological Report Recommendations

**Source:** bwu.neuro.reports.recs.aggression

## Overview

Aggression is identified as one of Jonathan's most significant behavioral and emotional problems, encompassing both physical and verbal hostile or destructive behaviors. Children with aggression problems often show deficits in problem-solving, perspective-taking, identifying alternatives, considering consequences, and determining causality.

## Full List of Recommended Intervention Strategies

- Bully Prevention
- Child-Centered Play Therapy
- Classroom Social Dynamics
- Cognitive Restructuring
- Counseling Groups
- Good Behavior Game (GBG)
- Incremental Theory Training
- Mindfulness-Based Interventions
- Peer-Mediated Conflict Resolution and Negotiation
- Problem-Solving Training
- Replacement Behavior Training
- [[concepts/social-skills-training]]
- Verbal Mediation

## Intervention Option 1: Problem-Solving Training

Teaches children a sequential, deliberate process for handling negative social situations. Goal is to develop new thinking patterns and behaviors to reduce aggression.

### Essential Elements
1. Recognize a problem exists
2. Define the problem accurately
3. Generate possible solutions
4. Evaluate pros and cons of each solution
5. Design, implement, and evaluate the chosen solution

### Key Procedural Phases

**PREP:** Identify aggression triggers; gather prior examples to guide discussion.

**IMPLEMENT:**
- Children identify and share a school-related aggression problem (anonymized).
- Define the problem from multiple perspectives.
- Generate at least three alternative solutions (written on a board for open discussion).
- Evaluate solutions collaboratively; list costs/benefits; cross out ineffective options.
- Practice via role-play or discussion.
- Apply the plan to real situations and review outcomes at the next session.
- Teach generalization using the **ICE acronym**: **I**dentify the problem, **C**reate alternatives, **E**valuate options.
- Set up a reinforcement plan (1:1 ratio for new appropriate behaviors).

**EVALUATE:**
- Children demonstrate the process independently.
- Report back on ICE outcomes.
- Identify situations where ICE can be applied.
- Rate the program; monitor aggression incidence.

## Intervention Option 2: Replacement Behavior Training

Focuses on teaching new, socially acceptable behaviors to replace aggressive ones. The replacement behavior is *functionally equivalent* — it serves the same purpose (e.g., escape/avoidance, tangible reward, stimulation) as the aggressive behavior.

### Essential Elements
1. Conduct a [[concepts/functional-behavioral-assessment]] to define the behavior and identify its function.
2. Identify and teach a replacement behavior that is equally accessible and yields the same outcome.
3. Reinforce the replacement behavior immediately; ignore the inappropriate behavior.
4. Evaluate and revise as needed.

### Key Procedural Phases

**PREP:**
- Assess child's cognitive/developmental readiness.
- Operationally define the target behavior.
- Assign responsibility for FBA data collection.
- Administer FBA; hypothesize the function of the behavior.
- Test hypothesis by introducing the antecedent.
- Select and plan reinforcement for the replacement behavior.

**IMPLEMENT:**
- Introduce replacement behavior training concept to the child.
- Teach the replacement behavior via direct instruction if appropriate.
- Use prompts or agreed-upon signals to cue the replacement behavior in context.
