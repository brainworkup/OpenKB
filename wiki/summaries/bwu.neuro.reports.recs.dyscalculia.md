# Recommendations: Dyscalculia

**Source:** bwu.neuro.reports.recs.dyscalculia

## Overview

This document provides a structured set of evidence-informed recommendations for parents and teachers supporting a student identified with [[concepts/dyscalculia]]. Recommendations span foundational numeracy, automaticity building, conceptual instruction, accommodations, and real-world generalization.

## Key Recommendation Areas

### Foundational Counting and Number Sense
- Use concrete activities (e.g., placing objects into egg carton holes) to build **rote counting** and **one-to-one correspondence**.
- Practice skip-counting by 2s, 3s, 4s, 5s, 6s, etc. to reinforce multiplication as repeated addition and support [[concepts/numeracy-foundations]].

### Visual-Spatial Organization
- Provide **graph paper** to assist with alignment of multi-digit numbers, compensating for poor [[concepts/visual-organizational-skills]].
- Use **visual cues** to teach operations involving sequencing or serialization.

### Math Fact Automaticity
- Teach memory strategies and tricks (e.g., finger trick for multiplying by 9).
- Use **mnemonics**, rhymes, flash cards, and digital games with immediate feedback.

### Hands-On and Manipulative Instruction
- Incorporate **manipulatives**, drawing, and hands-on activities when teaching mathematical concepts.
- Use physical demonstrations for relational concepts such as mass (e.g., bags filled with Styrofoam, sand, or beans for heavier/lighter comparisons).

### Money and Real-World Skills
- Attach coins to index cards with illustrative pictures to support coin identification and recall.
- Generalize math skills to real-world contexts: shopping, making change, cooking, estimating travel time.

### Accommodations
- Allow use of a **calculator** on tests/assignments assessing procedural knowledge rather than computational skill.
- Provide **extended time** on math tests and quizzes.

### Intervention
- Enroll in an [[concepts/evidence-based-intervention]] targeting the student's specific areas of weakness.
- Monitor progress closely; escalate to a more intensive intervention if adequate progress is not made.
