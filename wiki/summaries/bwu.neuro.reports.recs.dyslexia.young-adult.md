# Young Adult Dyslexia — Recommendations

This document provides structured recommendations for young adults diagnosed with [[concepts/dyslexia]] and ADHD who are returning to or attending college. It covers academic accommodations, [[concepts/strategic-reading]] techniques, writing strategies, and test-taking skills.

## School Accommodations

- Apply for formal school supports based on diagnoses of ADHD and reading disorder.
- **Extended time** on tests (×1.5 or ×2.0).
- Option to sub-vocalize multiple-choice questions.
- Testing in low-stimulus or isolated rooms.
- Access to professor outlines or PowerPoint slides prior to class.
- Right to audio-record lectures for later review.
- Access to audiobooks/text tapes for all textbooks.
- Use of dictation software (e.g., Dragon Naturally Speaking) at home and school.

## Strategic Reading Techniques

A core recommendation is to never read an assignment linearly from the start. Instead, follow a structured sequence:

1. **Skim first**: Read title, subtitles, graphs, introduction, and conclusion before the lecture.
2. **Attend lecture** and take notes.
3. **Review notes** after the lecture.
4. **Thoroughly read** the full assignment as soon as possible after the lecture.
5. **Annotate**: Take notes, write in margins, or use color-coded highlighting for main ideas vs. specific details.
6. Supplement with Cliffs Notes or critiques for complex texts.

For class discussions (rather than lectures), complete skimming, reading, and note-taking *before* the discussion.

### Active Reading Strategies (Home)

- Highlight and underline key points; use electronic highlighting tools for digital resources.
- Pause every 1–2 paragraphs to summarize aloud; re-read if unable to summarize.
- Answer in-chapter comprehension questions.
- Use Post-It notes to flag study guide answers for quick retrieval.

## Writing Strategies

- **Brainstorm** by dictating initial thoughts into an audio recorder or smartphone.
- Create an **outline** with thesis and sub-topics before drafting.
- Plan each paragraph on a single page listing needed ideas and facts.
- Draft on a computer.
- **Proofread in three separate passes**:
  1. Content and logical sequencing of ideas.
  2. Eliminate redundancy; simplify awkward sentences by reading aloud.
  3. Correct spelling and grammar using software tools.

## Lecture and Note-Taking Strategies

- Experiment with taking notes on a computer versus handwriting.
- Stay after class to review and supplement lecture notes.
- Recording lectures reduces the cognitive burden of simultaneous listening and writing — important for individuals with [[concepts/dyslexia]] and attention difficulties related to ADHD.

## Test-Taking Strategies

- Survey the entire test first to budget time appropriately.
- Read questions carefully and attend to details.
- Answer easiest or immediately known questions first.
- Prioritize high-point-value items (e.g., essays).
- Skip stuck items and return after completing the rest.
- For essays, quickly outline main ideas in the margins using bullet points.
