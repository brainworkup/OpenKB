# Dyslexia Recommendations — Pediatric Neuropsychology Report (Age 7)

Source: `bwu.neuro.reports.recs.dyslexia`
Tags: dyslexia, ADHD, reading, npsych, peds

## Overview

This document compiles clinical recommendations from an older neuropsychological report written for a 7-year-old child with [[concepts/dyslexia]] and comorbid [[concepts/adhd-clinical-features]]. Recommendations are organized by domain: medical/healthcare, school, home/family, college, reading skill-building, speech/language, and school/home environment. The child presents with phonological decoding difficulties, reduced [[concepts/working-memory]], attention and [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]], graphomotor weaknesses, and expressive language delays.

## Core Diagnoses Addressed

- **Dyslexia** (provisional at age 7; confirmatory eval recommended at Grade 3): [[concepts/phonological-processing]] deficits, poor decoding of unfamiliar/low-frequency words, slow reading speed, orthographic processing weaknesses
- **ADHD**: sustained attention difficulties, impulsivity (rushing through work), distractibility, executive function challenges
- **Expressive language / speech** delays (see [[concepts/speech-language-development-disorders]])
- **Graphomotor processing** difficulties under timed conditions (see [[concepts/dysgraphia]])

## Medical / Healthcare Recommendations

- Refer to an **educational therapist** (see [AET](https://www.aetonline.org/)) to manage school demands and reading
- Consult [International Dyslexia Association (IDA)](https://dyslexiaida.org/) and [Learning Disabilities of America (LDA)](https://ldaamerica.org/) for resources
- Consider **stimulant medication trial** to support [[concepts/reading-fluency]] given co-occurring ADHD

## School Accommodations

- **Extended time** on tests and quizzes (1.5x–2x); small/distraction-reduced testing environment (see [[concepts/test-taking-accommodations]])
- Reduced-distraction seating (not necessarily front — individualize)
- Allow keyboard/computer use instead of handwriting for written expression (see [[concepts/writing-accommodations]])
- Do not penalize spelling errors on content-area tests (history, science) — part of [[concepts/iep-accommodations]]
- Skills trainer/monitor serving as liaison between student and teachers
- IEP evaluation initiation recommended
- Preferential seating; anticipatory guidance for upcoming assignments
- Break assignments into sequential "mini-assignments" with feedback at each step
- Study buddy for note sharing and homework support
- **Pre-warn** child the day before any required oral reading in class; allow practice
- Schedule demanding tasks in the morning; allow frequent movement breaks
- Provide external organizational scaffolding (previewing tasks, commentary during, review after)
- Paraphrase/repeat-back strategy after teacher directions (see [[concepts/adhd-classroom-interventions]])

### Reading-Specific School Strategies

- Focus on [[concepts/reading-fluency]] over individual word analysis
- Techniques: imitative reading, repeated reading, radio reading, phrase reading, paired reading, echo reading
- Combined with sight-word recognition, decoding, [[concepts/vocabulary-development]], and comprehension
- Scaffold reading by discussing subject matter before reading, pre-reading end-of-chapter questions
- Monitor reading fluency and comprehension progress daily/weekly with graphed data
- Provide high-interest, low-readability books for pleasure reading
- Maintain a list of books read; reward achieving pre-set reading milestones

## Home / Family Recommendations

### Active Reading Strategies

- Highlight/underline main points; use electronic highlighting tools
- Pause every 1–2 paragraphs to summarize aloud; re-read if unable to summarize
- Answer in-chapter questions; use Post-It notes for study guide answers

### Memory & Study Strategies

- **Mnemonic devices** and visual imagery for new information
- **Chunking**: group pieces of information into larger units (e.g., phone numbers)
- Link new information to prior knowledge to leverage [[concepts/working-memory]]
- Spaced, repeated study sessions rather than massed cramming
- Flashcards with color-coding by subject; graduated introduction of facts to mastery

### Test-Taking Strategies

- Survey entire test first to budget time
- Do easiest items first; prioritize high-point items
- Skip and return to difficult items
- Outline essay answers in margins with bullet points

### Daily Reading Routine (Home)

- Parent reads aloud to child ≥15 minutes daily
- Child selects books (mix of easy, "just right," and challenging)
- Take turns reading
- Consistent homework routines: same time, same place, same subject order

## Reading Interventions

### Phonics

- Intensive, explicit phonics program (minimum 3–4x/week; daily preferred)
- Connect phonemes to orthography concretely; build automaticity before progressing
- Recommended approaches: [[concepts/orton-gillingham-reading-intervention]], Reading Recovery, Lindamood-Bell

### Orthography

- **Seeing Stars Program** for orthographic processing
- 2x/week writing practice: letters, words, copying from books, writing messages
- Writing strengthens motor cortex allographic letter representations and phoneme mapping
- Explicit attention to capitalization rules

### Reading Comprehension

- **PALS-R** (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies in Reading; Fuchs & Fuchs)
- Note: comprehension deficits at age 7 may be difficult to separate from decoding challenges

### Building Phonological Awareness

- Word games: rhyming, blending, sound deletion, syllable segmentation
- Use letter cards to build and manipulate words
- [[concepts/dialogic-reading]] (shared reading with adult-prompted questions)
- Orthographic awareness game: spell same word multiple ways maintaining same pronunciation

### Fluency / Motivation

- Record child reading difficult passages; child listens back
- Follow-along listening to recorded textbooks
- Maintain child's motivation; reward effort during protracted failure periods

## Speech, Language & Motor

- **Speech-Language Pathology (SLP)**: address articulation and expressive language (see [[concepts/speech-language-development-disorders]])
  - Direct vocabulary instruction, sentence modeling, grammar activities
- **Oral expression**: encourage ordering food, asking store clerks questions; positive conversational feedback; story-from-picture tasks
- Provide simple explicit grammar cues (reflect and model, do not require repetition)
- **Occupational Therapy**: graphomotor efficiency; right-hand/finger coordination; right-left confusion

## College-Level Recommendations

- Apply for disability accommodations: extended time (1.5x–2x), low-stimulus test room, sub-vocal reading option (see [[concepts/test-taking-accommodations]])
- Strategic reading: skim before lecture → attend lecture → read fully after lecture → annotate
- Writing process: dictate brainstorm → outline → draft on computer → three-pass proofreading (content/sequence, wordiness, spelling/grammar)
- Record lectures; request professor PowerPoints/outlines in advance
- Use dictation software (e.g., Dragon); access textbook recordings/tapes
