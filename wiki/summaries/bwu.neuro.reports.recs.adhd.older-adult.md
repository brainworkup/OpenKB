# ADHD in Older Adults

**Source:** bwu.neuro.reports.recs.adhd.older-adult

## Overview

This document addresses clinical recommendations and research limitations surrounding [[concepts/adhd-treatment-recommendations]] for older adults. It highlights a significant gap in pharmacological evidence for this population, relevant to the broader topic of [[concepts/adhd-older-adults]].

## Key Points

### Medication Options
- Adults with ADHD may benefit from both **FDA-approved stimulant** and **non-stimulant medications**.
- The same general pharmacological options apply to older adults, but with important caveats.

### Research Gap in Older Adults
- Older adults are **underrepresented** in pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic studies.
- Clinical trials for ADHD medications have frequently **excluded individuals over age 65**.
- Exclusion rationale: older adults tend to have **comorbid medical conditions** and **polypharmacy** concerns, which introduce confounding variables into study results.

### Clinical Implications
- The lack of systematic research leaves unanswered questions about:
  - **Effectiveness** of stimulants in older adults
  - **Tolerability** and safety profiles in this population
- Clinicians must rely on **limited available research** combined with clinical judgment when treating older adults with ADHD.
