# PAI Clinical Interpretive Report — Daniel Estey-Ang (DEA2003)

**Test Date:** 11/29/2023 | Language: English
**Author:** Leslie C. Morey, PhD and PAR Staff

## Validity of Test Results

- Completed items within acceptable limits (0 missing items).
- **Response consistency concern:** Some inconsistency between early and late items; possible attentional drift toward end of test. Results should be reviewed cautiously.
- **Negative impression management:** Subtle indicators suggest the client may have portrayed himself in a more pathological manner in certain areas. Patterns resemble profiles of individuals feigning mental disorder (see [[concepts/malingering-assessment]]).
  - Malingering Index: 57T; Rogers Discriminant Function: 66T; Hong Malingering Index: 70T; Multiscale Feigning Index: 73T
  - Fake bad coefficient of fit: .754; NIM predicted profile: .741; Random responding: .660
- **Back Random Responding:** 76T — elevated, suggesting possible inconsistency in latter responses.
- **No evidence of positive impression management** (defensiveness).
- Clinical scale elevations may overrepresent actual pathology.
- See also [[concepts/validity-and-response-styles]] and [[concepts/validity-language]].

## Clinical Features

### Cognitive/Perceptual

- Significant thinking and concentration problems; thought confusion, distractibility, and possible thought blocking.
- Active psychotic symptoms (hallucinations/delusions) **not** a prominent feature at this time, though one critical item endorsed hearing voices (SCZ-P item 170, ST).
- Profile fits most closely with schizophrenia (coefficient .859) and PTSD (.745) among diagnostic groups.
- See [[concepts/psychosis-clinical-features]] for related interpretive notes.

### Somatic Concerns

- Marked preoccupation with physical functioning; reports impairment across neurological, gastrointestinal, and musculoskeletal systems.
- Symptoms consistent with both conversion disorder and somatization disorder (see [[concepts/somatic-symptom-disorder]]).
- Somatic preoccupations dominate social interactions and self-image.

### Depression

- Significant depressive features, primarily **cognitive** in nature: negative expectancies, low self-esteem, worthlessness, hopelessness, pessimism.
- Affective and physiological components play a minimal-to-moderate role.
- See [[concepts/major-depressive-disorder-clinical-features]].

### Anxiety

- Moderate anxiety; phobic behaviors present but not severely impairing.
- Ruminative, detail-oriented, somewhat rigid.
- See [[concepts/anxiety-clinical-features]].

### Paranoia / Interpersonal Sensitivity

- Easily insulted; holds grudges; attributes misfortunes to others' neglect.
- Envious; disinclined to assist others.
- See [[concepts/paranoia-and-suspiciousness]].

### Borderline Features

- History of intense, volatile relationships.
- Preoccupation with fears of abandonment or rejection.
- See [[concepts/borderline-personality-disorder]].

### Mood / Personality Style

- Some elevated and variable mood; may appear active, ambitious, impatient, demanding.
- Adventurousness, risk-taking, impulsivity noted.

### Areas Without Significant Problems

- Empathy deficits: **not reported**.
- Alcohol or drug abuse/dependence: **not reported** (though two ALC critical items were endorsed; see [[concepts/substance-use-clinical-assessment]]).

## Self-Concept

- Generally negative self-evaluation with fluctuation between harsh self-criticism and periods of relative confidence.
- During stress: self-critical, pessimistic, dwelling on failures, indecisive.
- Tends to blame himself for setbacks; views future success as dependent on others.

---

## Interpersonal and Social Environment

- Very uncomfortable in social situations; passive, submissive interpersonal style.
- Little interest in social interaction; socially isolated.
- Perceives social relationships as offering little support; family relationships distant.
- Views environment as relatively stable despite low social support.

---

## Treatment Considerations

- **Motivation for treatment:** Adequate; acknowledges problems and expresses desire for help; values therapy and personal responsibility.
- **Treatment challenges:**
  - Therapeutic relationship will carry special weight given weak social support network.
  - Difficulty trusting treatment professionals.
  - May be too disorganized or overwhelmed for some treatment modalities.
  - Likely resistant to psychological framing of problems (somatic attribution).
- Anger management: within normal range.
- Suicidal ideation: not currently reported as distressing (but Suicide Potential Index = 81T — notable discrepancy).
