# Academic Accommodations in Higher Education

Academic accommodations in higher education are formal, institutionally sanctioned modifications to the standard learning environment granted to students with documented disabilities. They are designed to remove barriers to learning without altering the core academic standards or expectations of a course or program.

For students with [[concepts/dyslexia]], [[concepts/adhd-clinical-features]], reading disorders, and related neurodevelopmental conditions, these accommodations are often essential for equitable participation in college-level coursework.

## How Accommodations Are Obtained

Students must typically:
1. Disclose their diagnoses to the college's disability services office.
2. Provide documentation — such as a neuropsychological evaluation report — substantiating their diagnoses.
3. Request specific accommodations that are formally approved and communicated to instructors.

Sharing a copy of one's neuropsychological report with the institution is often encouraged to facilitate this process (see [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.dyslexia.young-adult]]).

## Common Accommodations for Dyslexia and ADHD

### Testing Accommodations
- **Extended time**: Students may receive 1.5× or 2.0× the standard time on tests and exams. This addresses slow reading fluency and the additional cognitive load associated with [[concepts/dyslexia]] and attention difficulties.
- **Reduced-distraction testing environment**: Tests are administered in low-stimulus rooms or individually, minimizing the impact of attentional difficulties on performance.
- **Sub-vocalization option**: On multiple-choice questions, students may read questions aloud quietly to themselves to support comprehension.

See also [[concepts/test-taking-accommodations]].

### Instructional Accommodations
- **Lecture recording**: Students may audio-record lectures for later review, alleviating the dual cognitive demand of listening and note-taking simultaneously — a particularly significant burden for individuals with [[concepts/working-memory]] difficulties.
- **Pre-class materials**: Professors may provide outlines or PowerPoint slides before class, allowing students to preview content and annotate during the lecture rather than transcribing from scratch.
- **Audiobooks and text tapes**: Access to audio versions of textbooks significantly increases reading comprehension for students with reading disorders.

### Assistive Technology
- **Dictation software** (e.g., Dragon Naturally Speaking): Allows students to compose written work by speaking, bypassing some of the challenges associated with [[concepts/dysgraphia]] and slow written output.
- **Electronic highlighting tools**: Support active reading and annotation in digital texts.
- **Computer-based note-taking**: May be more efficient than handwriting for students with written expression difficulties.

See [[concepts/writing-accommodations]] for more detail on writing-specific supports.

## Relationship to Learning Profiles

Accommodations are not one-size-fits-all; they are tailored to the individual's specific profile of strengths and weaknesses. For example:

- A student with [[concepts/dyslexia]] and slow [[concepts/reading-fluency]] benefits most from extended time and audiobook access.
- A student with [[concepts/adhd-clinical-features]] may particularly benefit from low-distraction testing and pre-class lecture notes.
- A student with combined reading disorder and ADHD (a common co-occurrence) may require the full range of supports described above.

Strategic reading and writing strategies — such as those outlined in [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.dyslexia.young-adult]] — complement formal accommodations by building the student's own skill set alongside institutional supports.

## Complementary Strategies

Accommodations work best when paired with evidence-based learning strategies:
- [[concepts/strategic-reading]]: Structured pre/during/post reading routines.
- [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]]: Organizational and planning strategies for writing and studying.
- [[concepts/attention-intervention-strategies]]: Techniques for managing attention during lectures and reading.
- [[concepts/iep-accommodations]]: The K–12 precursor framework that may inform a student's existing accommodation history.
