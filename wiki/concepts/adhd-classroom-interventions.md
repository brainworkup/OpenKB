# ADHD Classroom Interventions

ADHD classroom interventions are structured, evidence-informed strategies used by teachers and school staff to support students who exhibit difficulties with attention, impulse control, emotional regulation, and abstract reasoning. These interventions are typically recommended in neuropsychological or psychoeducational reports and are calibrated to the severity of the student's difficulties, often using T-score thresholds derived from standardized rating scales. When ADHD co-occurs with [[concepts/dyslexia]] or other learning disorders, classroom strategies must address both attentional and academic skill dimensions simultaneously.

## Core Domains of Intervention

### Attention and Working Memory

Students with elevated scores on attention and [[concepts/working-memory]] indices benefit from environmental modifications and task restructuring:

- **Task modification**: Shorten assignments to match attention span; break long tasks into smaller steps; give one assignment at a time; assign every third problem rather than all items.
- **Instruction delivery**: Provide both written and oral instructions; post or send homework assignments home; ensure eye contact when giving directions; have student paraphrase instructions to confirm comprehension.
- **Self-monitoring tools**: Use visible timers; assist with short-term goal setting; use private cueing signals; teach students to check their own work before submitting.
- **Environmental accommodations**: Strategic seating near teacher, role model, or peer study buddy; increase physical distance from distracting peers; allow soft background music for some students.
- **Time accommodations**: Allow extra time for tasks and tests (1.5x–2x); schedule demanding tasks in the morning when attention is strongest; allow frequent movement breaks.
- **Anticipatory guidance**: Use day planners or calendars; cue students 10 minutes before the end of a period; help students prepare for upcoming assignments and projects.

These strategies align with broader [[concepts/iep-accommodations]] frameworks and support students with [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]].

---

### Impulse and Activity Control

Tiered strategies apply depending on severity:

**At T ≥ 60 (mild-to-moderate elevation):**
- Increase the immediacy of rewards and consequences
- Praise and reinforce compliant behavior; acknowledge nearby students' positive behavior
- Ignore minor inappropriate behavior; use prudent reprimands; avoid lecturing
- Provide closer supervision during transitions
- Teach self-monitoring (e.g., for calling out); call on student only when hand is raised appropriately
- Encourage proofreading of work a few hours or days after initial completion; offer partial credit for self-corrections

**At T ≥ 70 (clinically significant elevation):**
- Implement a formal classroom [[concepts/behavior-modification]] system
- Use weekly/monthly calendars with visual reinforcement markers (e.g., stickers)
- Gradually increase the interval before reinforcement is delivered
- Use a hierarchy of rewards, from small immediate rewards to preferred activities for sustained compliance

This tiered approach reflects principles of reinforcement scheduling and positive behavior support.

---

### Compliance and Anger Control

For students showing frustration, anger dysregulation, or social withdrawal:

**At T ≥ 60:**
- Monitor for stress buildup and proactively reduce workload
- Speak softly and non-threateningly; spend more individual time with the student
- Reinforce frequently when frustration signs are observed
- Encourage student to self-identify anger and seek an adult
- Provide leadership roles and special responsibilities to build positive peer perception
- Monitor social interactions; encourage peer engagement for withdrawn students
- Maintain frequent parent conferences to leverage student interests and strengths

**At T ≥ 70:**
- Formal behavior management system targeting positive emotional expression
- Calendar-based reinforcement with gradual fading of intervals
- Reward hierarchy for sustained emotional regulation

---

### Abstract and Conceptual Reasoning

For students with difficulties in organization and higher-order thinking:

- Use highlightable texts
- Teach explicit strategies for forming and organizing conceptual ideas
- Use sticky notes to externalize and spatially organize ideas before writing
- Use weekly/monthly planners; review regularly with student
- Break assignments into ordered, explicit steps
- Involve parents in monitoring homework organization
- Continually review use of logic and reasoning in completed work
- Help student appreciate the "big picture" before engaging with details; ask student to restate the task in their own words and develop a solution plan

These strategies support students with [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]] and complement [[concepts/iep-accommodations]] for learning-related weaknesses.

## Comorbid Dyslexia and ADHD: Dual-Focus Interventions

When ADHD co-occurs with [[concepts/dyslexia]], classroom strategies must address both domains. The dyslexia component requires specific reading and writing accommodations layered onto attention management supports.

### Reading Accommodations
- Allow extended time on all reading-based tests and quizzes
- Administer high-stakes tests in a reduced-distraction, small-group or individual setting
- Do not penalize spelling errors on content-area tests (history, science, etc.)
- Provide recorded textbooks and allow follow-along listening
- Scaffold reading by discussing subject matter before the reading, pre-reading end-of-chapter questions and boldfaced headings
- Pre-warn the student the day before any required oral reading in class; allow practice with a tutor or parent
- Assign a study buddy who can share lecture notes

### Written Expression Accommodations
- Allow use of keyboard or computer instead of handwriting for written assignments
- Use spell-check, word prediction, speech recognition, and graphic organization software
- Do not count spelling errors against the student on written expression tasks
- Ask the student what they wrote whenever handwriting is unclear; consider use of a recording device

See [[concepts/writing-accommodations]] and [[concepts/test-taking-accommodations]] for additional frameworks.

### Reading Skill-Building in the Classroom
- Focus instruction on [[concepts/reading-fluency]] rather than isolated word analysis
- Use fluency techniques: imitative reading, repeated reading, radio reading, phrase reading, paired reading, and echo reading
- Build [[concepts/phonological-processing]] through word games: rhyming, blending, sound deletion, syllable segmentation
- Use evidence-based programs: Orton-Gillingham approaches (see [[concepts/orton-gillingham-reading-intervention]]), Lindamood-Bell, Seeing Stars (orthographic processing)
- Implement PALS-R (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies in Reading; Fuchs & Fuchs) for comprehension
- Monitor reading fluency and comprehension progress daily or weekly; graph data to inform intervention
- Provide high-interest, low-readability books for pleasure reading; maintain a reading log with rewards
- Use [[concepts/dialogic-reading]] (shared reading with adult-prompted questions) for early literacy and vocabulary

### Engagement and Motivation
- Vary the instructional medium and pace to sustain interest
- Emphasize hands-on, active learning activities
- Emphasize accuracy over speed; provide separate grades for accuracy and speed on assignments
- Reward effort, not just outcomes — particularly important for students experiencing protracted reading difficulties
- Keep open communication with the student about their difficulties to build self-awareness and acceptance

## Organizational and Executive Support

For students with ADHD ± learning disorders who struggle with organization and sustained effort:

- Assign a **skills trainer/monitor** who serves as a liaison between student and all teachers; schedule regular check-ins at the beginning and end of each week
- Initiate an **IEP evaluation** and integrate neuropsychological findings; see [[concepts/iep-accommodations]]
- Share neuropsychological report with school staff
- Provide external organizational scaffolding: preview tasks before starting, offer commentary during, and review after completion
- Consider a stimulant medication trial to support both attention and reading fluency in comorbid ADHD + dyslexia cases
- Schedule most demanding tasks in the morning; allow frequent breaks

## Tiered Severity Model

| T-Score Range | Interpretation | Intervention Level |
|---|---|---|
| < 60 | Within normal limits | No specific recommendation |
| 60–69 | Mildly elevated | Tier 1: Environmental & behavioral support |
| ≥ 70 | Clinically significant | Tier 2: Formal behavior management systems |

This model aligns with standard psychometric conventions and supports tiered multi-level intervention planning.

## Clinical Context

These strategies are embedded within neuropsychological reports generated as part of a [[concepts/neuropsychological-reporting]] workflow. They are indexed to the D-REF (Diagnostic Rating and Evaluation Form), a behavioral rating tool used to quantify ADHD-related difficulties across multiple domains. The D-REF indices map closely to [[concepts/adhd-clinical-features]] including inattention, hyperactivity/impulsivity, and emotional dysregulation.
