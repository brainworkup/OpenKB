# YC Application Planning

## Overview
YC application planning is the process of identifying application prompts, organizing them by section, distinguishing required from optional materials, and using that inventory to guide completion of the submission. In this wiki, the concept is grounded by [[summaries/YC Application Questions_20260413233900]], [[summaries/YC Application Questions_20260416091345]], [[summaries/application_20260413205413]], and [[summaries/application_20260413212452]]. Together, these sources show both the high-level YC application structure and the specific checkpointing workflow used to track unfinished answers.

The application form itself provides the canonical structure: team, product, location, progress, strategy, legal, and YC-specific motivation. The question-tracking summaries then operationalize that structure into a manageable list of remaining work. As a result, YC application planning is both a map of the form and a system for moving a draft toward completion.

## Core Idea
The main function of YC application planning is to turn a large application into a manageable task list without losing the strategic logic of the application. Rather than treating the submission as one long writing exercise, this approach breaks it into discrete prompts across progress, strategy, market, legal, founder materials, and supporting artifacts. This supports clearer sequencing, faster drafting, and better coordination with related work such as [[concepts/application-question-tracking]], [[concepts/application-preparation]], and [[concepts/startup-application-materials]].

The structural sources, [[summaries/application_20260413205413]] and [[summaries/application_20260413212452]], reinforce that this is not just a writing exercise but a constrained submission system. YC defines required fields, optional uploads, file-size limits, and post-submission editing restrictions. That means planning must account not only for what to say, but also for what artifacts must exist, what can be deferred, and what becomes harder to change later.

In that sense, YC application planning is a practical system for measuring completion. It converts the application into a finite set of required answers, optional supporting materials, and logistical dependencies such as founder profile completion, founder video, product link, demo, location explanation, and possible coding-session export.

## Structure of Remaining Work
Based on [[summaries/YC Application Questions_20260413233900]] and [[summaries/YC Application Questions_20260416091345]], the remaining work was organized into four groups. [[summaries/application_20260413205413]] and [[summaries/application_20260413212452]] help clarify how those groups sit inside the full YC form and what supporting constraints apply to each.

### Progress
The sources identify four unanswered questions in the progress section:
- How far along are you?
- How long have the founders been working on this, and how much of that time has been full-time?
- Are people using the product?
- What tech stack is being used or planned?

The full application structure also shows that YC explicitly asks applicants to include AI models and AI coding tools in the tech stack, and offers an optional coding agent transcript export. This makes the progress section partly about product status and partly about technical execution evidence. It overlaps with founder narrative and execution credibility, making it relevant to founder narrative, [[concepts/founder-track-record]], and [[concepts/ai-code-generation]] when technical implementation is part of the story.

The structural sources also make clear that progress is not isolated from assets: product link, credentials, demo video, and coding-session export can all support the credibility of the written progress answers. In planning terms, this means progress work may require parallel artifact preparation rather than only text drafting.

### Strategy and Market
The sources identify four grouped questions in strategy and market:
- Why this idea?
- Do the founders have domain expertise?
- How do they know people need it?
- Who are the competitors, and what is uniquely understood?
- How will the company make money, and how much could it make?
- Which category best fits the company?

Although counted as four higher-level YC prompts, these include several subparts. Together they define the application's market logic and differentiation case. This area relates closely to [[concepts/application-strategy]], [[concepts/competitive-landscape-analysis]], [[concepts/startup-differentiation]], [[concepts/market-sizing]], [[concepts/founder-evaluation]], and [[concepts/solo-founder-strategy]] where relevant.

[[summaries/application_20260413205413]] and [[summaries/application_20260413212452]] clarify that this section is where the application must connect founder insight to market proof: not just what the company does, but why this team chose the idea, what they understand about competitors, and how that understanding supports a revenue opportunity. They also show that category selection is part of the planning problem, since the application asks for a single category best describing the company.

### Legal
The sources identify three unanswered legal questions:
- Whether any legal entity has been formed
- Whether any investment has been taken
- Whether the company is currently fundraising

These questions are operationally simple but strategically important because they frame company maturity and financing status. They connect to [[concepts/startup-fundraising]].

The full YC form also shows that legal and financing questions sit alongside history questions about previous applications, pivots, and incubator participation. Even when not part of the counted open-questions list, those surrounding fields matter for planning because they affect consistency across the company story and may require factual verification before submission.

### Optional Items
The sources also note two optional items:
- Coding agent session attachment
- Other ideas considered for applying

These items are not required, but they can strengthen the overall application if they add evidence of technical capability or thoughtful founder judgment. The coding session attachment is especially related to [[concepts/coding-agent-session]].

The structural sources add useful detail here: the coding agent session is an experimental Summer 2026 prompt, intended to let applicants show off skill with AI coding tools, and must be uploaded as a .md or .txt file under 25 MB. They also distinguish optional demo material from required founder material, which is important when deciding how to allocate effort.

## Key Takeaway from the Source
The main planning value across the checkpoint documents is the explicit count of remaining work:
- 11 required unanswered questions
- 2 optional items

That count creates a concrete planning baseline. It converts vague application progress into a measurable completion problem, which is the practical heart of YC application planning. The structural sources, [[summaries/application_20260413205413]] and [[summaries/application_20260413212452]], confirm why this works: the application is highly structured, so progress can be tracked reliably against named sections and submission requirements.

The sources also add several planning-relevant constraints:
- founder profile must be complete before submission,
- founder intro video is required,
- founder video file size is limited to 100 MB,
- demo video is optional but limited to 3 minutes and 100 MB,
- coding-session export is optional and constrained by file type and 25 MB size limit,
- product link and credentials may need to be prepared together,
- location must be given in a specific current-location / post-YC-location format, and
- after submission, only certain sections remain editable.

These details strengthen the concept by showing that planning is not only about drafting answers. It is also about sequencing dependencies, deciding which optional artifacts are worth producing, and making sure hard-to-edit sections are complete before submission.

## Why This Concept Matters
YC applications reward clarity, compression, and evidence. Planning helps ensure that:
- every required prompt is answered,
- the full form structure is understood before drafting begins,
- high-leverage sections are recognized early,
- the founder story stays consistent across sections,
- optional materials are evaluated strategically rather than added impulsively,
- supporting assets such as video, links, credentials, and exports are prepared on time, and
- operational constraints such as profile completion, upload limits, required formatting, and edit restrictions are handled in advance.

In practice, this concept sits at the intersection of task management and narrative design. It is not only about finishing forms; it is about assembling a coherent startup case across team, product, progress, market, legal status, location, and YC-specific motivation. The repeated checkpoint structure seen in the source summaries suggests that good YC application planning is iterative: review the open questions, recategorize what remains, compare the current draft against the canonical application structure, and use the count of unanswered prompts to drive the next drafting pass.
