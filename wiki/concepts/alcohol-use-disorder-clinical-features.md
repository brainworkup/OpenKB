# Alcohol Use Disorder — Clinical Features

Alcohol Use Disorder (AUD) is characterized by a problematic pattern of alcohol use leading to clinically significant impairment or distress. In psychological assessment contexts, AUD is evaluated through self-report measures, structured clinical indices, and behavioral observation, with particular attention to severity, functional impact, and response style factors that may affect accurate reporting.

## Diagnostic Criteria (DSM-5)

The DSM-5 defines AUD along a severity continuum (mild, moderate, severe) based on the number of diagnostic criteria met within a 12-month period. Core features include:

- **Loss of control** over alcohol consumption (inability to cut down, drinking more than intended)
- **Craving** and preoccupation with obtaining or using alcohol
- **Continued use despite consequences** — interpersonal, occupational, and health-related
- **Tolerance** (needing more to achieve the same effect)
- **Withdrawal** symptoms upon cessation
- **Functional impairment** across major life domains

Severe AUD is coded **303.90 / F10.20** in DSM-5/ICD-10 systems.

## PAI Assessment of AUD

The [[concepts/personality-assessment-inventory]] includes a dedicated **ALC (Alcohol Problems) scale** with subscales tapping both current problematic use and historical consequences. Elevated ALC scores are among the most clinically significant findings the PAI can yield.

### Key Assessment Findings — Ed Gonzalez (EG24)

In [[summaries/pai_317]], the ALC scale showed the **only significant clinical elevation** in the profile:

- Respondent endorsed **trouble controlling alcohol use** (Item 55, ALC: Mainly True)
- Endorsed that his **drinking has gotten him into trouble** (Item 334, ALC: False — reverse-keyed, scored as significant)
- ALC Estimated Score was **30T lower than actual ALC score** — a large discrepancy suggesting possible **underreporting** or defensiveness around alcohol
- DSM-5 diagnostic possibility raised: **Alcohol Use Disorder, Severe** (303.90 / F10.20)

All other clinical scales were within normal limits, making AUD the **singular focal concern** in this profile.

## Functional Impact Domains

AUD-related impairment commonly spans:

| Domain | Typical Consequences |
|---|---|
| **Interpersonal** | Relationship conflict, social withdrawal or over-reliance |
| **Occupational** | Absenteeism, performance decline, job loss |
| **Medical/Health** | Liver disease, neurological effects, sleep disruption |
| **Legal** | DUI, domestic incidents |
| **Psychological** | Co-occurring depression, anxiety, trauma sequelae |

## Response Style Considerations

Accurate assessment of AUD is frequently complicated by **positive impression management** and defensiveness. Individuals with AUD may:

- Minimize or deny the extent of use and consequences
- Present a socially desirable profile on validity scales
- Show a large gap between estimated and actual ALC scores

In [[summaries/pai_317]], the Defensiveness Index (63T) and Hong Defensiveness Index (67T) were mildly elevated, and the ALC estimated score was 30T below the actual score — consistent with this pattern. See [[concepts/validity-and-response-styles]] for broader context on how response distortion affects clinical interpretation.

## Comorbidity and Differential Considerations

AUD frequently co-occurs with:

- [[concepts/depression-clinical-features]] — depressive episodes often precede or follow heavy use
- [[concepts/anxiety-clinical-features]] — alcohol may be used to self-medicate anxiety
- [[concepts/trauma-informed-clinical-assessment]] — trauma histories are common in AUD populations
- [[concepts/somatic-symptom-disorder]] — physical complaints may be alcohol-related

In this case, co-occurring conditions were **not endorsed** by the respondent, though defensiveness may have suppressed reporting.

## Treatment Motivation

A critical challenge in AUD treatment is low motivation for change. When an individual does not perceive their use as problematic — or actively minimizes consequences — engagement in treatment is reduced. In [[summaries/pai_317]], the Treatment Process Index (55T) and low RXR scores suggest **below-average treatment motivation**, consistent with a pattern of self-satisfaction and minimization common in AUD presentations.

See [[concepts/treatment-motivation-and-compliance]] for discussion of motivational factors in treatment planning.
