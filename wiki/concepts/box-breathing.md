# Box Breathing (Square Breathing)

Box breathing — also called square breathing or four-square breathing — is a controlled breathing technique that uses equal-duration phases of inhalation, breath-holding, exhalation, and rest. It is widely used to reduce acute stress, improve concentration, and anchor mindful awareness to the present moment.

## The Technique

The protocol follows a simple four-step cycle, each phase lasting four seconds:

1. **Breathe in** — 4 seconds
2. **Hold** — 4 seconds
3. **Breathe out** — 4 seconds
4. **Rest** — 4 seconds

This cycle is repeated as needed until a sense of calm and focused attention is achieved. The symmetric structure (hence "box" or "square") is easy to remember and does not require any equipment or special environment.

## Mechanisms and Benefits

- **Stress reduction:** Slow, deliberate breathing activates the parasympathetic nervous system, counteracting the physiological stress response.
- **Improved concentration:** Directing attention to the breath cycle displaces distracting thoughts, making it easier to refocus on tasks.
- **Mindfulness facilitation:** The rhythmic, sensory nature of the practice provides a concrete anchor for present-moment awareness — a key goal of [[concepts/mindfulness-based-interventions]].
- **Emotional regulation:** By slowing arousal, box breathing can help interrupt impulsive or reactive behavior patterns.

## Clinical Applications

Box breathing is particularly recommended for individuals with:

- **ADHD** — helps manage inattention, restlessness, and difficulty sustaining focus. It is explicitly recommended in ADHD young adult clinical guidance (see [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.adhd.young-adult]]).
- **Anxiety** — complements other anxiety management strategies; see [[concepts/anxiety-clinical-features]].
- **Executive function difficulties** — supports self-regulation and cognitive control; see [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]].

## Relationship to Mindfulness

Box breathing is often introduced as a gateway practice for individuals new to mindfulness. It provides a structured, concrete entry point into breath-focused attention before progressing to open-awareness or body-scan practices. See [[concepts/mindfulness-based-interventions]] for broader context on mindfulness applications in clinical populations.

Box breathing requires no specialized training, tools, or cost. It can be practiced anywhere and is appropriate for all ages, making it a highly accessible self-regulation tool for diverse clinical populations.
