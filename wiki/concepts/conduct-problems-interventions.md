# Conduct Problems Interventions

Conduct problems interventions refer to the set of evidence-based strategies used to prevent and remediate conduct disorder in children — a condition characterized by a persistent pattern of behaviors that violate the rights of others or major societal norms. These interventions address the significant resistance to change typical of conduct problems, often stemming from the intrinsically rewarding nature of antisocial behaviors.

See the primary source: [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.conduct-problems]]

---

## Why Conduct Problems Require Specialized Intervention

- Conduct problems are among the most clinically significant behavioral and emotional problems in children
- The coercive cycle between parent and child behavior tends to self-reinforce without structured intervention
- Early prevention (for at-risk children) and active treatment (for identified cases) are both critical to interrupt long-term disorder progression
- Resistance to change makes multi-modal and sustained approaches necessary

Related clinical concerns include [[concepts/aggression-interventions]], [[concepts/anger-management]], and [[concepts/antisocial-personality-features]].

## Evidence-Based Intervention Menu

Multiple approaches have demonstrated effectiveness for conduct problems:

| Intervention | Focus |
|---|---|
| Anger Management Skills Training | Emotion regulation |
| Independent Group Contingency Management | Behavioral incentives |
| Interdependent Group Contingency Management | Shared behavioral incentives |
| Moral Motivation Training | Values and empathy |
| Multimodal Interventions | Combined approach |
| Multisystemic Therapy | Family and systems level |
| **Parent Training** | Parent behavior and skills |
| Problem-Solving Training | Cognitive flexibility |
| **Social Skills Training** | Prosocial behavior |

## Intervention 1: Social Skills Training

[[concepts/social-skills-training]] is a cognitive-behavioral approach that teaches prosocial skills as functional alternatives to maladaptive conduct behaviors.

### Goal
Build social competency to prevent and remediate conduct problems in at-risk children.

### Essential Elements
1. Assess social skills deficits
2. Model and explicitly teach appropriate skills
3. Practice in contrived (role-play) scenarios
4. Provide reinforcement and corrective feedback
5. Generalize skills to real-world situations

### Implementation Highlights
- One skill introduced per session, supported by visual steps
- Brainstorming, modeling, and peer coaching used in each session
- Journals used to track skill application and outcomes
- Prior skills reviewed at the start of each new session
- Teachers and parents receive skill steps for monitoring and generalization

See also: [[concepts/behavior-modification]], [[concepts/self-monitoring-behavior]]

## Intervention 2: Parent Training

[[concepts/parent-training]] is a psychoeducational (social learning) intervention targeting the **coercive parent-child interaction cycle** — where escalating directives and negative responses reinforce antisocial behavior.

### Goal
Increase effective parenting skills and positive disciplinary techniques to reduce antisocial behavior.

### Skill Areas Taught to Parents
- Reinforcement strategies (verbal praise, social, tangible, activity reinforcers)
- Observation and play skills
- Response-cost and timeout procedures
- Punishment and extinction principles
- Relationship enhancement (communication, problem-solving, partner support)
- Token economy and reward charts; contingency contracts
- Mood management, self-determination, and relaxation techniques
- Anger management and self-monitoring

### Implementation Highlights
- Can be delivered in individual or group format
- Intake assessment evaluates family climate, barriers, and goals
- One technique taught per session with description, modeling, and role-play
- Homework assigned to practice techniques independently between sessions
- Each session opens with review of the previous technique and homework debrief

See also: [[concepts/functional-behavioral-assessment]], [[concepts/clinical-guidelines]]

---

## Procedural Framework (Both Interventions)

Both Social Skills Training and Parent Training follow a shared three-phase structure:

1. **PREP** — Assessment, goal-setting, logistics, and group/individual format decisions
2. **IMPLEMENT** — Skill introduction, modeling, role-play, coaching, and homework
3. **EVALUATE** — Monitoring via teacher/parent reports and direct observation

This framework aligns with structured behavioral intervention principles described in the BASC-3 Behavior Intervention Guide.
