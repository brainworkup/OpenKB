# Orton-Gillingham and Structured Literacy Interventions

Orton-Gillingham (OG) and related Structured Literacy approaches are evidence-based, multisensory, sequential, and explicit instructional methods designed to remediate [[concepts/dyslexia]] and related [[concepts/phonological-processing]] deficits. They are among the most widely recommended interventions in pediatric neuropsychological reports for children presenting with reading disorder.

See also: [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.dyslexia]], [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.build-reading-skills]]

## Core Principles

- **Explicit instruction**: Phoneme-grapheme correspondences are taught directly and systematically, not incidentally
- **Multisensory engagement**: Simultaneous use of visual, auditory, and kinesthetic-tactile pathways (e.g., tracing letters while saying sounds)
- **Sequential and cumulative**: Skills build on one another; simpler patterns before complex
- **Diagnostic and prescriptive**: Instruction is individualized based on ongoing assessment
- **Mastery-based**: Students do not advance until current material is automatic

## Relationship to Dyslexia

[[concepts/dyslexia]] is characterized by difficulty with accurate and/or fluent word recognition, poor spelling, and poor decoding ability — rooted in [[concepts/phonological-processing]] deficits. Orton-Gillingham and Structured Literacy approaches directly target these core deficits by:

1. Teaching the alphabetic principle explicitly
2. Building phoneme awareness and phoneme-grapheme mapping
3. Developing orthographic knowledge (letter patterns, syllable types)
4. Progressing toward reading fluency and comprehension

## Major Programs and Variants

### Orton-Gillingham (OG)
The foundational approach, developed by Samuel Orton and Anna Gillingham in the 1930s. OG is a framework rather than a scripted curriculum, implemented by trained reading specialists or educational therapists. Recommended explicitly in pediatric neuropsychological reports for phonics-based reading remediation.

### Reading Recovery
An early literacy intervention (typically Grade 1) using one-on-one tutoring to support struggling readers. Recommended alongside OG in neuropsychological reports as an intensive early option.

### Lindamood-Bell (LMB)
A specific structured literacy program emphasizing phoneme awareness through articulatory feedback (Lindamood Phoneme Sequencing, or LiPS) and visualization-verbalization for comprehension. Recommended as an alternative option for phonics-based remediation.

### Seeing Stars Program (Symbol Imagery)
A Lindamood-Bell program targeting **orthographic processing** — the ability to form and recall mental images of letter sequences. Specifically recommended for children with weaknesses in orthographic coding, complementing phonological approaches. See [[concepts/dyslexia]] for orthographic processing context.

### PALS-R (Peer-Assisted Learning Strategies in Reading)
Developed by Lynn and Douglas Fuchs; a structured, evidence-based peer tutoring program targeting early reading comprehension. Recommended for use alongside phonics intervention once decoding has been partially established.

## Recommended Intensity and Timeline

From clinical neuropsychological practice (see [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.dyslexia]]):

- **Minimum**: 3–4 sessions per week
- **Optimal**: Daily intervention
- **Duration before progressing**: Approximately 6 months of intensive phonics work before shifting emphasis to oral reading fluency and comprehension
- **Sequence**: Phonics automaticity → [[concepts/reading-fluency]] → comprehension

## Phonological Skill-Building Activities

Even outside formal OG sessions, phonological awareness can be built through:

- **Rhyming games**: Identifying and generating rhyming words (real and nonsense)
- **Sound blending**: Pushing individual phonemes together into words
- **Syllable segmentation**: Clapping out syllables, then breaking into phonemes
- **Sound deletion**: Removing a phoneme from a word to form a new word
- **Letter card manipulation**: Building words and changing letters/combinations
- **Orthographic spelling games**: Spelling a word multiple ways that maintain the same pronunciation (builds knowledge of letter-phoneme correspondences)

## Orthographic and Writing Supports

Writing reinforces phoneme-grapheme mapping and strengthens motor cortex allographic representations:

- 2x/week writing practice: letters, words, copying from books
- Writing family names, favorite items, shopping lists
- Explicit capitalization instruction (beginning of sentences, proper nouns)
- Use of [[concepts/dysgraphia]]-aware supports when graphomotor difficulties co-occur

## Fluency Instruction Techniques

Once basic phonics is established, [[concepts/reading-fluency]] is addressed through:

- Imitative reading
- Repeated reading
- Radio reading
- Phrase reading
- Paired reading
- Echo reading
- Combined with sight-word recognition, vocabulary development, and comprehension

## Integration with Other Interventions

Orton-Gillingham and Structured Literacy approaches are most effective when integrated with:

- [[concepts/iep-accommodations]]: Extended time, reduced-distraction environment, spelling exemptions on content tests
- [[concepts/test-taking-accommodations]]: 1.5x–2x extended time, low-stimulus rooms
- [[concepts/writing-accommodations]]: Keyboard/computer use, spell-check, word prediction software, dictation tools (e.g., Dragon)
- [[concepts/adhd-classroom-interventions]]: When co-occurring ADHD affects sustained attention during reading instruction
- [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]] supports: Breaking reading tasks into sequential steps, monitoring progress
- [[concepts/speech-language-development-disorders]] therapy: When expressive language or articulation delays co-occur with dyslexia
- [[concepts/dialogic-reading]]: Shared reading intervention to build early literacy and vocabulary
- [[concepts/vocabulary-development]]: Explicit vocabulary instruction, word logs, flashcards
- [[concepts/working-memory]] supports: Chunking, mnemonic strategies, linking new to known information

## Progress Monitoring

- Collect data on reading fluency and comprehension daily or weekly
- Graph data to visualize progress and set appropriate literacy goals
- Adjust intervention based on monitored progress
- Maintain lists of books read; reward milestones to sustain motivation
