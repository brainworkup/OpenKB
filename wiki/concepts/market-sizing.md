# Market Sizing

## Definition
[[concepts/market-sizing]] is the process of estimating the size of a target market so a company can explain who it serves, how large the opportunity is, and how that opportunity could expand over time.

## Context in Luria’s YC application
In [[summaries/README_20260413235353]], [[summaries/README_20260413235533]], and [[summaries/README_20260414001057]], market sizing appears as one of the core research pillars supporting Luria’s YC Summer 2026 application. Across these summaries, market size is framed not as an isolated finance exercise, but as part of a broader application narrative that also includes [[concepts/application-strategy]], [[concepts/founder-narrative]], [[concepts/startup-differentiation]], [[concepts/regulatory-positioning]], and [[concepts/coding-agent-session]].

The newer README summary strengthens this framing by presenting market sizing as part of a complete application research foundation: alongside YC partner preferences, [[concepts/solo-founder-strategy]], [[concepts/competitive-landscape-analysis]], regulatory framing across FDA/HIPAA/APA contexts, local-LLM technical viability via MLX, and question-by-question tactical guidance for the application. In that context, market sizing helps answer YC-style questions about company scope, initial wedge, and long-term venture potential.

## Key details from the sources
The source summaries state that the research foundation includes:
- a core TAM of **$18–27M**
- an expanded market opportunity of **$150–220M**

Across the README summaries, this appears as a deliberate two-layer market argument:
1. a narrowly defined initial market with a credible near-term use case
2. a broader adjacent or future market that supports a venture-scale growth story

The newer summary also makes clear that these figures are part of a coordinated application strategy rather than a standalone estimate: they sit beside claims about no direct competitors, no YC portfolio overlap, regulatory positioning, technical feasibility, and tactical preparation for the application itself.

## Why market sizing matters here
For these documents, market sizing serves several strategic purposes:
- shows that the startup opportunity is large enough to justify investor attention
- helps make the company legible to YC partners evaluating scale potential
- anchors claims about focus, sequencing, and product wedge
- supports a story in which the company starts with a specific neuropsychological AI use case and expands outward over time
- complements competitive and technical claims by showing not just that the product is differentiated, but that the market is worth entering

In this sense, [[concepts/market-sizing]] is closely tied to [[concepts/startup-fundraising]] and [[concepts/founder-evaluation]] because market size is often used as a proxy for whether a startup could become significant.

## Interpretation of the two market ranges
The distinction between **$18–27M core TAM** and **$150–220M expanded TAM** implies deliberate segmentation.

### Core TAM
The smaller range likely represents the most immediate, clearly addressable market for the first product offering. In the Luria context, this supports a focused go-to-market story: start with a well-defined problem, specific users, and concrete workflow value.

This kind of estimate is especially useful in an application setting because it signals discipline. Rather than claiming an unrealistically massive market from day one, the company can show it understands the boundaries of its first usable market.

### Expanded TAM
The larger range likely represents adjacent markets or broader categories that become reachable after proving the initial wedge. This is a common startup framing: begin with a constrained entry point, then expand into a wider platform or workflow footprint.

This logic supports [[concepts/startup-differentiation]] because it shows both discipline and ambition: focused enough to launch, large enough to matter.

## Role in YC-style storytelling
In application terms, market sizing helps answer implicit YC questions such as:
- Is this a real business opportunity?
- Is the team starting with a sharp wedge?
- Could this become much bigger than its first use case?

The README summaries collectively suggest that market sizing is not merely descriptive; it is rhetorical. It helps the founder tell a coherent story about why this company should exist now, why the initial market is believable, and why expansion is plausible.

The newer summary sharpens this by placing market sizing inside a question-by-question application strategy that also covers [[concepts/yc-partner-preferences]], solo-founder positioning, and the [[concepts/coding-agent-session]]. In that framing, market sizing becomes part of how the founder demonstrates judgment: not just that the market is large, but that the founder understands where to start and how to grow.

That makes [[concepts/market-sizing]] part of a broader application-prep toolkit alongside [[concepts/application-preparation]], [[concepts/yc-partner-preferences]], and [[concepts/startup-application-materials]].
