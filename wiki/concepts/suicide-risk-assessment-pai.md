# Suicide Risk Assessment and Intervention via PAI

The Personality Assessment Inventory (PAI) provides structured, empirically grounded indicators for identifying suicide risk in clinical and forensic contexts. When critical item endorsements, scale elevations, and supplemental risk indices converge, the PAI generates explicit intervention recommendations — including the need for **immediate suicide intervention** — that must be integrated with clinical judgment and collateral information.

## Role of the PAI in Suicide Risk Detection

The PAI does not produce a single "suicide score" but instead triangulates risk through multiple converging sources:

- **Critical item endorsement** — specific items flagged for self-harm ideation and intent
- **Clinical scale elevations** — particularly on Depression (DEP), Borderline Features (BOR), and Anxiety (ANX) scales
- **Supplemental risk indices** — dedicated experimental indices for suicide and violence risk appearing in the Additional Profile Information section
- **Profile fit coefficients** — matching the respondent's overall profile against known high-risk clinical groups

See [[concepts/suicide-and-violence-risk-indices]] for a detailed account of these supplemental measures.

## Case Profiles: Documented Suicide Risk Presentations

### Corey Ho (pai_96) — Moderate Risk, MDD/Identity Profile

In the PAI report for Corey Ho ([[summaries/pai_96]]), the Treatment Considerations section identifies significant suicide risk requiring careful clinical follow-up based on:

- **Suicide Potential Index T = 68** — elevated, representing a significant warning sign independent of other scale levels
- **Critical item endorsement:**
  - Item 100 (SUI): "I've made plans about how to kill myself" — Slightly True (1)
  - Item 206 (DEP-A): "I have no interest in life" — Slightly True (1)
- Compounding risk factors including:
  - Hopelessness, anhedonia, and worthlessness characteristic of a major depressive episode (see [[concepts/depression-clinical-features]])
  - Significant anxiety and physical tension impairing concentration (see [[concepts/anxiety-clinical-features]])
  - Marked identity disturbance and fragile, relationship-dependent self-esteem (see [[concepts/identity-disturbance-clinical-features]])
  - Cognitive disorganization — confusion, distractibility, and difficulty concentrating
  - Passive, retiring interpersonal style (see [[concepts/social-avoidance-and-withdrawal]])
  - Poorly established self-concept with harsh self-criticism (see [[concepts/unstable-self-concept]])
  - Possible trauma history (items 34 and 114 from ARD-T endorsed)

**Profile fit data for this case:**
- Highest diagnostic group fits: Major depressive disorder (0.757), Persistent depressive disorder (0.751), Schizoaffective disorder (0.729), Anxiety disorders (0.725)
- Symptom behavior group fits: **Current suicide (0.737)**, Antipsychotic medications (0.727), Auditory hallucinations (0.682), **Suicide history (0.652)**, Self-mutilation (0.621)
- Context-specific fit: Motor vehicle accident claimants (0.744); poor fit with law enforcement officer candidates (-0.591) and child custody evaluations (-0.507)

**Protective factors in this case:**
- Treatment motivation typical of clinical patients; acknowledges need for help and values personal change (see [[concepts/treatment-motivation-and-compliance]])
- Stress level and social support approximately average compared to normal adults
- No significant substance use reported; violence potential within normal range
- Validity indicators suggest forthright responding

**Complicating factors:**
- Inattention Index T = 77 — elevated, may reflect genuine cognitive difficulties or disorganization
- May currently be too disorganized or overwhelmed to participate meaningfully in some forms of treatment
- RXR Estimated Score 7T higher than RXR — possible limited openness to change despite stated motivation
- Level of Care Index T = 61 — modestly elevated, suggesting structured support may be warranted

**DSM-5 diagnostic possibilities suggested:** Major depressive disorder, single episode, unspecified (296.20/F32.9); rule out cyclothymic disorder, panic disorder, and borderline personality disorder.

**Complicating factors:**
- May be too disorganized or overwhelmed to meaningfully participate in some forms of treatment

**DSM-5 diagnostic possibilities suggested:** Major depressive disorder (single episode), Generalized anxiety disorder, Persistent depressive disorder (dysthymia); rule out schizophrenia, social anxiety disorder, and other specified personality disorder with mixed borderline/avoidant features.

## Cross-Case Patterns in PAI Suicide Risk

Across the cases documented in this wiki, several consistent patterns emerge in how the PAI captures suicide risk:

1. **Suicide Potential Index (SPI) T ≥ 68** consistently triggers explicit clinical warnings, regardless of other scale elevations.
2. **Profile fit with "Current suicide" symptom behavior group** above 0.42 (statistically significant) appears in all moderate-to-high risk cases.
3. **Critical item 206** (DEP-A: "I have no interest in life") is a recurring endorsement across multiple cases with elevated suicide risk.
4. **Depression, borderline features, and identity disturbance** frequently co-occur with elevated suicide risk, forming a core vulnerability cluster.
5. **Cognitive disorganization** — whether indexed by Inattention, confusion, or distractibility — may both signal distress severity and complicate treatment engagement.
6. **Treatment motivation** appears relatively intact across most cases despite significant risk, which is an important prognostic anchor.
