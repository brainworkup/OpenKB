# Regulatory Positioning

## Overview
Regulatory positioning is the practice of describing a product so its capabilities, risks, compliance obligations, and intended use are aligned with the legal and professional frameworks that govern it. In the context of Luria’s YC application materials, this concept is treated as a core part of startup strategy rather than a secondary compliance detail. Across [[summaries/README_20260413235353]], [[summaries/README_20260413235533]], and [[summaries/README_20260414001057]], regulatory positioning across FDA, HIPAA, and APA frameworks is presented as one of the central pillars of the YC S26 application research foundation.

## Why it matters
For a neuropsychological AI product, regulatory positioning affects:
- how the product is described to investors and accelerators
- what claims can be made safely and credibly
- whether the product is understood as workflow support, clinical documentation support, assessment support, or something closer to diagnosis or treatment
- how privacy, clinician oversight, and risk controls are communicated
- whether the company appears thoughtful about operating in a sensitive clinical domain

This makes regulatory positioning tightly connected to [[concepts/startup-differentiation]], [[concepts/application-strategy]], and [[concepts/founder-evaluation]]. In healthcare, a startup can appear more credible when it shows it understands not only the problem and market, but also the operational boundaries of responsible deployment. In the YC framing, this helps answer whether the founders have the judgment to build ambitiously without making reckless clinical or legal claims.

## Main dimensions of regulatory positioning

### FDA-related positioning
FDA positioning concerns whether the product could be interpreted as a regulated medical device or software performing functions that trigger medical-device oversight. For an AI system in neuropsychology, this often depends on:
- intended use
- claims made in product and fundraising materials
- degree of clinical autonomy
- whether the system supports versus replaces clinician judgment

A careful position generally emphasizes assistance, workflow support, summarization, data organization, or draft generation rather than autonomous diagnosis or treatment recommendation. This aligns well with existing Luria-related concepts such as [[concepts/clinical-ai-copilot]], [[concepts/neuropsychological-assessment-automation]], and [[concepts/clinical-narrative-generation]].

Within a YC application, this kind of framing also matters strategically: accelerator partners are likely to respond better to a product that is ambitious but legible, where intended use is narrow enough to be credible and broad claims are avoided. The application materials suggest that regulatory framing is meant to shape not only compliance posture but also how the company explains its product category, boundaries, and roadmap.

### HIPAA-related positioning
HIPAA positioning concerns how protected health information is handled, stored, transmitted, and secured. In practice, this means the company must be able to explain:
- what data it receives
- where data is processed
- how access is controlled
- what de-identification or redaction steps exist
- whether local processing reduces exposure risk

This connects directly to [[concepts/clinical-data-privacy]], [[concepts/phi-data-handling]], [[concepts/phi-deidentification-pipeline]], [[concepts/pii-redaction-pipelines]], and [[concepts/privacy-first-software]]. The newer README’s explicit mention of local-LLM viability via Apple MLX strengthens the link between regulation and architecture by suggesting that privacy-sensitive deployment choices are not only theoretical but technically plausible.

The addition of [[summaries/README_20260414001057]] makes this clearer: local inference is part of the product’s application story because it can support a stronger privacy and data-minimization narrative while also demonstrating concrete technical feasibility.

### APA and professional-practice positioning
APA-related positioning is less about statutory regulation and more about professional norms, ethics, scope, and acceptable use in psychological practice. For a neuropsychological product, this includes:
- respecting clinician responsibility
- preserving report quality and interpretive caution
- avoiding overclaiming diagnostic certainty
- fitting into established clinical workflows

This dimension connects with [[concepts/clinical-guidelines]], [[concepts/neuropsychological-reporting]], [[concepts/neuropsychological-assessment-workflow]], and [[concepts/report-review-qa]]. It also supports the startup’s effort to appear aligned with how neuropsychologists actually work, rather than merely applying generic AI automation language to a clinical setting.

In the YC context, this professional-practice alignment also helps the founder present the company as careful about scope: useful to clinicians, compatible with real workflows, and not framed as replacing licensed judgment.

## Relationship to product architecture
Regulatory positioning is not only a messaging issue; it shapes technical design choices. The source materials link regulatory planning with local-LLM feasibility, suggesting that architecture can reinforce compliance claims. For example:
- local processing may support [[concepts/privacy-first-software]] and [[concepts/local-first-architecture]]
- constrained model roles may support [[concepts/clinical-ai-copilot]] framing rather than autonomous clinical decision-making
- review layers may support safer [[concepts/clinical-ai-reasoning]] and [[concepts/report-review-qa]]
- on-device or tightly controlled inference may strengthen claims about minimizing PHI exposure

This also relates to [[concepts/local-llm-inference]], [[concepts/mlx-framework]], and [[concepts/local-inference-reliability]]. The explicit reference to Apple MLX in [[summaries/README_20260413235533]] and [[summaries/README_20260414001057]] makes the architecture-regulation connection more concrete: local inference is part of both the technical story and the compliance story.

The newer README also reinforces that this technical framing is being used in application preparation, not just internal system design. In other words, architecture is being used as evidence for a regulatory and trust narrative that YC reviewers can understand quickly.

## Role in YC application strategy
Within the YC context, regulatory positioning helps answer an implicit investor question: does this team understand how to build in a sensitive domain without stepping into avoidable risk? In that sense, it supports:
- clearer founder judgment
- stronger market credibility
- more believable go-to-market sequencing
- defensible differentiation in healthcare AI
- better alignment between product ambition and practical deployment constraints

In [[summaries/README_20260413235353]], [[summaries/README_20260413235533]], and [[summaries/README_20260414001057]], regulatory positioning is treated as one of several application-critical workstreams, alongside founder positioning, competitive analysis, technical viability, and market sizing. The most recent README goes further by framing the work as part of question-by-question tactical preparation for the YC application and by noting coverage of the new Coding Agent Session. That suggests regulatory positioning is meant to influence not just one answer but the tone, claims, and boundaries of the entire submission.

This places it within broader [[concepts/application-preparation]] and [[concepts/startup-application-materials]]. It also connects to founder-background prompts indirectly: strong accomplishment and product answers become more persuasive when paired with evidence that the founder understands how to operate responsibly in a regulated domain.

## Practical takeaway
Regulatory positioning is the disciplined framing of what a healthcare AI product is, what it is not, and how it fits within legal and professional boundaries. For Luria, as described in [[summaries/README_20260413235353]], [[summaries/README_20260413235533]], and [[summaries/README_20260414001057]], it is a strategic asset used to make the startup legible to YC: ambitious, clinically relevant, privacy-aware, technically grounded, and careful about claims.
