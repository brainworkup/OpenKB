# Goal Setting and Self-Monitoring

Goal setting and self-monitoring are foundational strategies within [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]] intervention, helping individuals — particularly children with attention or regulatory challenges — develop the internal scaffolding needed to plan, execute, and evaluate their own behavior and performance.

## Core Concepts

**Goal Setting** involves identifying specific, realistic targets for behavior or task completion and committing to a plan for reaching them. Effective goals are:
- Concrete and measurable
- Broken into sub-goals when the task is complex
- Developed collaboratively with the individual to build investment

**Self-Monitoring** is the process by which an individual observes, tracks, and evaluates their own progress toward a goal. It shifts locus of control inward, building metacognitive awareness and independence over time.

## Clinical Application

From [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.executive-fx]], the following goal-setting and self-monitoring strategies are recommended:

### Task and Time Management
- Teaching the use of a **schedule or calendar** to record deadlines, appointments, and homework
- Allowing the child to monitor their own progress using a **timing device**
- Having the child estimate how long tasks will take, building an internal sense of time

### Breaking Down Tasks
- Large or multistep assignments should be decomposed into **smaller, manageable steps**
- Realistic sub-goals are set for each step
- Progress toward each sub-step is monitored incrementally

### Contract Approach
- A **behavioral contract** developed collaboratively with the child establishes expectations and monitors progress
- Involving the child in contract development increases their investment in outcomes
- This approach supports independence and self-direction

### Responsibility and Follow-Through
- Clear rules and expectations around timeliness are established and maintained
- Children are encouraged to take ownership of completing and turning in assignments

## Relationship to Executive Functioning

Goal setting and self-monitoring are closely tied to [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]], particularly the sub-domains of:
- **Planning and organization** — decomposing tasks, sequencing steps
- **Working memory** — holding goals in mind while executing steps (see [[concepts/working-memory]])
- **Cognitive flexibility** — adjusting goals based on feedback (see [[concepts/cognitive-flexibility-training]])
- **Inhibition** — resisting distraction to stay on-task

These skills are also central to [[concepts/self-monitoring-behavior]], [[concepts/attention-intervention-strategies]], and broader [[concepts/adhd-treatment-recommendations]].

## Mindfulness and Delay of Gratification

Supporting practices such as [[concepts/mindfulness-based-interventions]] and learning to delay gratification reinforce the executive control systems that make self-monitoring possible. Children who can tolerate delayed rewards are better able to stay on task toward longer-horizon goals.
