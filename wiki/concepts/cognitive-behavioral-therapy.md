# Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT)

Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT) is a structured, evidence-based psychological treatment that addresses the interplay between thoughts, emotions, and behaviors. It is among the most widely supported interventions in clinical psychology and neuropsychology, with applications across a broad range of conditions.

## Core Principles

- **Cognitive restructuring**: Identifying and challenging maladaptive or distorted thought patterns.
- **Behavioral activation and modification**: Changing problematic behavioral patterns through structured exercises and exposure.
- **Skill-building**: Teaching patients practical coping strategies they can apply independently.
- **Time-limited structure**: CBT is typically delivered in a defined number of sessions with clear goals.

## Clinical Applications in Neuropsychological Contexts

### Conversion and Somatic Symptom Disorders
As outlined in [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.conversion]], CBT serves as the **second-line treatment** for patients with [[concepts/conversion-disorder]] or [[concepts/somatic-symptom-disorder]] who do not respond to education alone. The stepped-care model recommends:

1. **First line**: Patient education (e.g., resources at [www.neurosymptoms.org](http://www.neurosymptoms.org))
2. **Second line**: CBT when education is insufficient

This positions CBT as a critical escalation in the management of functional neurological symptoms.

### Other Relevant Applications
- **Depression**: CBT is a cornerstone intervention for [[concepts/depression-clinical-features]] and [[concepts/major-depressive-disorder-clinical-features]].
- **Anxiety**: Widely used for [[concepts/anxiety-clinical-features]].
- **ADHD**: Incorporated into adult ADHD treatment plans (see [[concepts/adhd-treatment-recommendations]]).
- **Anger management**: Core component of [[concepts/anger-management]] programs.
- **Conduct and aggression problems**: Referenced in [[concepts/aggression-interventions]] and [[concepts/conduct-problems-interventions]].
- **Borderline personality disorder**: Dialectical Behavior Therapy, a CBT derivative, is central to [[concepts/borderline-personality-disorder]] treatment.
- **Mindfulness integration**: Modern CBT variants incorporate mindfulness (see [[concepts/mindfulness-based-interventions]]).

## CBT in Neuropsychological Reporting

CBT recommendations often appear in [[concepts/clinical-guidelines]] sections of neuropsychological reports, particularly when cognitive or emotional dysregulation is identified. It frequently complements [[concepts/behavior-modification]] strategies and psychoeducation.
