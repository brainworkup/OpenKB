# Aging and Older Adulthood

Aging and older adulthood represent a distinct life stage increasingly recognized not merely as decline, but as a period with unique psychological, neurological, and personal dimensions. In clinical neuropsychology, understanding aging is essential for accurate assessment, appropriate intervention, and meaningful support of older patients.

## Core Ideas

- **Reframing aging:** Contemporary perspectives challenge deficit-focused narratives of old age, emphasizing that longer life can be accompanied by greater purpose, wisdom, and meaning.
- **Purposeful living:** Authors like Marc Agronin (*The End of Old Age: Living a Longer, More Purposeful Life*) argue that aging can be a stage of renewed engagement rather than passive decline. See [[concepts/purposeful-living]].
- **Cognitive considerations:** Aging involves changes in cognitive domains including processing speed, working memory, and executive function, which are central to neuropsychological evaluation. See [[concepts/cognitive-domains]], [[concepts/working-memory]], and [[concepts/mild-cognitive-impairment]].
- **Differential diagnosis:** Distinguishing normal age-related changes from pathological conditions (e.g., mild cognitive impairment, depression in late life) is a core clinical task.

## Clinical Relevance

- Neuropsychological assessments in older adults require age-normed scores and sensitivity to the interaction between aging and neurodevelopmental or psychiatric conditions.
- ADHD presentations in older adults represent an underrecognized clinical population, with dedicated resources such as *ADHD After Age 60* addressing diagnosis and treatment in seniors. See [[concepts/adhd-older-adults]].
- Age classification frameworks inform how clinicians stratify and report findings. See [[concepts/age-group-classification]].
- Anger management and emotional regulation challenges can persist or emerge in later life, with resources such as *When Anger Hurts: Quieting the Storm Within* addressing these needs. See [[concepts/anger-management]].
