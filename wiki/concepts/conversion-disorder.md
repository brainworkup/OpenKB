# Conversion Disorder (Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder)

Conversion disorder, also known as Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder (FND), is a condition in which patients experience neurological symptoms — such as weakness, paralysis, tremor, seizures, or sensory disturbances — that cannot be explained by a structural neurological disease or other medical condition. These symptoms are real and distressing to the patient, and are understood to arise from disrupted brain function rather than damage or disease.

## Relationship to Somatic Symptom Disorder

Conversion disorder is closely related to [[concepts/somatic-symptom-disorder]], and the two conditions are sometimes grouped together in clinical contexts. Both involve physical symptoms that are medically unexplained or disproportionate, and both benefit from similar treatment approaches. The distinction lies in the nature of the symptoms: conversion disorder specifically involves deficits in voluntary motor or sensory function suggestive of a neurological condition.

## Clinical Features

- Neurological symptoms (e.g., non-epileptic seizures, limb weakness, gait disturbance, vision or speech problems) without a consistent organic etiology
- Symptoms may fluctuate and are often influenced by attention, distraction, and stress
- Onset may follow a psychosocial stressor, though this is not required for diagnosis
- Significant functional impairment and distress
- Diagnosis typically requires neurological evaluation to rule out structural causes

## Treatment Recommendations

Clinical guidance for managing conversion disorder follows a stepwise approach (see [[summaries/bwu.neuro.reports.recs.conversion]]):

### First-Line: Patient Education
Education is the foundational first-line intervention. Patients are informed about the nature of their symptoms — that the brain is not sending or receiving signals correctly, rather than any structural damage being present. This framing reduces stigma and helps patients understand that recovery is possible.

- Accessible patient education materials are available at [www.neurosymptoms.org](http://www.neurosymptoms.org)

### Second-Line: Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy (CBT)
For patients who do not improve with education alone, [[concepts/cognitive-behavioral-therapy]] (CBT) is the recommended next step. CBT targets maladaptive thought patterns and behaviors that may perpetuate symptoms, and supports the development of coping strategies.

## Role in Neuropsychological Assessment

Conversion disorder may arise in neuropsychological contexts where examiners must differentiate functional symptoms from those caused by structural neurological conditions. This requires careful consideration of [[concepts/validity-and-response-styles]] and symptom presentation patterns. Related concerns include distinguishing conversion symptoms from malingering (see [[concepts/malingering-detection]]).
