# Social Skills Training

Social skills training (SST) is a **cognitive-behavioral intervention** approach that teaches children the prosocial skills needed to function successfully in their typical environments. It is commonly indicated for children with social competency deficits, which frequently co-occur with [[concepts/conduct-problems-interventions]], attention difficulties, and other behavioral or developmental concerns.

## Purpose and Goals

The primary goal of social skills training is to **prevent and remediate** components of behavioral disorders — particularly conduct problems — by providing children with prosocial alternatives to maladaptive behaviors. SST is used both as a prevention strategy for children at elevated risk and as a direct treatment for those already identified with social skill deficits.

SST is recognized as one of several evidence-based intervention strategies for conduct problems, alongside parent training, anger management, problem-solving training, and multisystemic therapy. See [[concepts/parent-training]] for a complementary family-focused approach.

## Essential Elements

Effective social skills training includes five core procedural elements:

1. **Assess deficits** — Determine specific social skills deficits through formal assessment, teacher and parent input, and child self-report.
2. **Model appropriate skills** — Demonstrate target behaviors via explicit explanation and behavioral modeling.
3. **Practice in contrived scenarios** — Apply learned skills in role-play and structured practice situations.
4. **Provide feedback and reinforcement** — Deliver corrective feedback and positive reinforcement for appropriate responses.
5. **Generalize to real situations** — Support transfer of skills to actual naturalistic contexts.

## Implementation Procedures

### Preparation
- Organize the schedule of meetings and form small groups of children with similar presenting problems.
- Establish clear performance goals, boundaries, rules, and consequences for participation.
- Identify and prioritize social skills deficits using assessments (e.g., BASC-3), teacher and caregiver recommendations, and child self-disclosures.

### Implementation
- **Introduce one skill per session** — Focus each meeting on a single target skill (e.g., effective listening) with clearly defined steps.
- **Use visual supports** — Write skill steps on notecards or visual displays; ask children to recite and practice steps.
- **Brainstorm and contextualize** — Engage children in discussion about real situations that required use of the new skill.
- **Model the skill** — Demonstrate the target behavior explicitly (e.g., making eye contact, taking a deep breath, verbalizing calmly).
- **Role-play with coaching** — Children practice the skill in simulated scenarios while peers or adults coach them; the facilitator may role-play an antagonist to create realistic practice conditions.
- **Review and maintain** — Begin each session by reviewing skills taught previously; ask about opportunities to use the skill and troubleshoot challenges.
- **Use journals** — Encourage children to record skill use and outcomes in journals, optionally shared with the group.
- **Hold refresher sessions** — Schedule periodic review sessions to maintain skill acquisition over time.

### Evaluation
- Distribute skill step cards to teachers and parents, asking them to monitor and record skill use.
- Schedule routine observations (e.g., weekly or monthly classroom walk-throughs) to track progress.

## Clinical Context

SST is grounded in behavioral and cognitive-behavioral principles, overlapping with [[concepts/behavior-modification]] and [[concepts/cognitive-flexibility-training]]. It is particularly relevant in neuropsychological practice when evaluating children with [[concepts/executive-function-deficits]], [[concepts/autism-spectrum-disorder-clinical-features]], or ADHD-related social difficulties.

Detailed procedural guidance for SST in the context of conduct problems is provided in the **BASC-3 Behavior Intervention Guide**. Related behavioral rating tools are discussed under [[concepts/behavioral-rating-scales]].
